# Labelled finite convex geometries and a thin uncountable infinitary class

## Objective and completion criterion

Develop a reusable library of finite closed diagrams, extension of bounded observations, countable realization, stable refinement, and infinitary comparison. Its principal application is the **specified construction**: a countable relational language \(L\) and a sentence \(\Phi\in L_{\omega_1,\omega}\) whose countable isomorphism classes number exactly \(\aleph_1\), with no perfect isomorphism antichain.

The organizing invariant is a **continuous decreasing family of expansion domains with countable successor losses and increasing logical agreement**. Do not require a total stopping rank, exhaustive rank layers, characteristic arity, a canonical terminal representative, or a Borel structure on the quotient of models. A second route to the main theorem organizes the count instead by least presentation levels of full presentations (the section "Reduction to full presentations"); both routes are retained.

The intended construction of the top-free witnesses (the terminal models at each countable stage, which give the lower bound; every other countable model is classified, not constructed) is to **construct the finite age and reconstruct its classical limit**, not to implement a specialized infinite construction: the development proves the finite hypotheses of the classical Fraïssé existence theorem for an age of finite labelled charts, quotes that theorem (available at the pin), and reconstructs a realization from the limit (the section "The intended construction" below). In order: finite chart geometry → a finite age with the hereditary, joint embedding, and amalgamation properties (HP, JEP, AP) → its classical Fraïssé limit → reconstruction of partial evaluation, giving a realization → receiving and modelhood → the expansion-domain filtration. Finite amalgamation is proved before any infinite model is constructed; it is not established by embedding charts into the model whose existence the Fraïssé theorem is to supply. This route replaces the specialized top-free chain construction, not the receiving and decoding mathematics or the terminal classification. It is the first of two reconstruction problems, kept apart throughout (the section "The intended construction"): finite-age reconstruction supplies the top-free witnesses, and higher-stage reconstruction (Layers 4–5) recovers the expansions of a model to later stages.

This README is the mathematical roadmap; `IMPLEMENTATION.md` refines it into an implementation order with semantic guards and acceptance checkpoints (with a table relating its numbering to the layers below), and prevails where the two differ. `Suggested.lean` and `SuggestedInterfaces.lean` record selected Lean statements and theorem targets; proving only the statements in those files does not complete the project. `SEMANTIC_CONTRACT.md` fixes the meanings that any reformulation must preserve. `EXPOSITIONS.md` gives three informal accounts, `LITERATURE.md` the connections with the literature (with `REFERENCES.bib`), and `HULL_ALGEBRA.md` the definitional expansion by hull operations, whose construction and closure equality belong to the core (Layer 2) and whose further consequences are optional. `COMPANIONS.md` states three optional companion milestones (filtration and infinitary theory, top-free chart homogeneity and its consequences, a geometric obstruction), with their Lean statements in `SuggestedCompanions.lean`; the main theorem does not depend on them. The amalgamation of top-free charts is step 2 of the core construction of the top-free witnesses. The correspondence with [Kni26] and [AFK26] is a required milestone spanning Layers 2–6, with its own completion criterion (the section "Manuscript correspondence (required)", and `IMPLEMENTATION.md`, "Manuscript concordance").

## Reduction of the main theorem to expansion domains

This is the first route to the main theorem; the second, the reduction to full presentations, follows this section. Let \(Q\) be the set of countable base-model isomorphism classes and
\[
\lambda_\xi=\omega+\omega\cdot\xi,\qquad
D_\xi=\{q\in Q:q\text{ admits a model expansion to }\lambda_\xi\}.
\]
Establish, for countable indices:

1. \(D_0=Q\), the domains decrease, and \(D_\delta=\bigcap_{\xi<\delta}D_\xi\) at nonzero countable limits.
2. Each successor loss \(D_\xi\setminus D_{\xi+1}\) is countable.
3. Models in \(D_\eta\) agree on every sentence of quantifier rank at most \(\eta\) (to be derived from the stronger back-and-forth form below).
4. Each successor loss is nonempty, by a separate top-free construction.

The first two imply countable domain complements. The third gives countable truth or falsehood sides for every sentence, and descriptive separation (invariant analytic separation and López–Escobar, in the form of the library's thinness theorem for countable sentence splits) turns these into thinness; descriptive separation is used for thinness only. For the upper bound, Scott separation (distinct classes are separated by a sentence) and the third condition make the persistent core \(\bigcap_{\eta<\omega_1}D_\eta\) a subsingleton, and its complement is covered by the \(\aleph_1\) many countable exceptions \(Q\setminus D_\eta\), so \(|Q|\le\aleph_1\). Morley's dichotomy would also give the upper bound from thinness; it is not used. The fourth gives the lower bound by disjointness of losses. The literal sentence correspondence and absence of finite models give the all-countable-carrier version. No global eventual-departure theorem occurs in this reduction: conditions 3 and 4, with a Scott sentence for each class, imply one (eventual departure, `COMPANIONS.md`, "Further companion results", terminal refinement), which empties the persistent core, but the count uses only its bound by one class.

**Condition 3 from back-and-forth** (a theorem to prove). *For every countable ordinal \(\eta\) and any two countable models \(M\) and \(N\) of \(\Phi\) whose isomorphism classes lie in \(D_\eta\), the empty tuples of \(M\) and \(N\) are back-and-forth equivalent at \(\eta\) in the base language: `BFEquiv η 0 ⟨⟩ ⟨⟩` (InfinitaryLogic's `BFEquiv`).* Proof plan: apply InfinitaryLogic's graded-matching theorem `bfEquiv_of_gradedMatching` (Layer 0), with height \(\eta\), to the relations \(R_\alpha\) of the match (ii) of Layer 0: \(R_\alpha\) holds of two \(n\)-tuples when \(\alpha\le\eta\) and there are model expansions of \(M\) and \(N\) to \(\lambda_\alpha\), a common chart (a stage type at \(\lambda_\alpha\) on \(k\) points) with an actual occurrence in each expansion (the two covers), and a selector \(\mathrm{Fin}\,n\to\mathrm{Fin}\,k\), repetitions and the empty tuple allowed, through which the two tuples factor (the expansions may be the reductions of fixed expansions to \(\lambda_\eta\), but the relation does not require it). The zero clause is the agreement of the base atomic diagrams of equal types at \(\lambda_0\) (Layer 2); descent reduces both expansions and the common chart to the lower stage (the reduction of models, Layer 2, coherent by `StageType.reduce_reduce`), so no uniqueness of expansions is used; the forth and back clauses are the one-block transfer of Layer 5 (finite-cut receiving of the target at the cutoff \(\lambda_\alpha\), with donor the type at \(\lambda_{\alpha+1}\) of a closed extension of the source containing the requested point). The forth and back clauses alone yield no related pair, so the **initial match**, \(R_\eta\) on the two empty tuples, is a separate statement to be proved, as two separate lemmas: existence, that the empty set is closed in both expansions, so that each has an actual occurrence of the empty chart (a property of the application, not of every structured geometry); and compatibility, that the two empty charts have the same type at \(\lambda_\eta\) (any two stage types on zero points are equal: `StageType.eq_of_zero`, compiled in this repository (theorem named)). Condition 3 then follows by `BFEquiv_implies_agreeQR` (InfinitaryLogic, available at the pin (signatures checked); relational language) with the same rank bound \(\eta\): it is a corollary of the back-and-forth form, not a separate induction on sentences. The back-and-forth form also gives thinness in scatteredness form (Layer 6). The passage from the match (ii) to `BFEquiv`, on abstract hypotheses for the expansions, the charts, and the covers, is `ExpansionMatchData.bfEquiv_of_expansionMatch` (`Comparison/GradedMatchingApplications`), compiled in this repository (theorem named), with existence and compatibility as its two separate hypotheses; its instantiation to the expansion domains (the one-block transfer, the reduction of models, the closedness of the empty set) and the two further applications, to sentences and to thinness, are not elaborated.

The first three conditions do not by themselves yield the lower bound. Nonempty domains alone are not a substitute for nonempty losses (with an empty persistent core they do give uncountability: `COMPANIONS.md`, terminal refinement, the lemma on nonempty domains with an empty persistent core). Nor does high-stage existence alone produce terminal witnesses.

**The filtration is the counting interface.** The construction supplies four things: terminal countability (condition 2, from the countable cover of terminal classes, Layer 4), comparison (condition 3, the one-block transfer, Layer 5), limit continuity (condition 1), and nonempty losses at every block (condition 4, the top-free witnesses; cofinally many would suffice for the lower bound). Generic counting and sentence separation finish the proof. The crucial step of the lower bound: a terminal expansion supplies a loss only after uniqueness of expansion and same-carrier transport exclude another, higher expansion of its base reduct (Layer 6). Eventual stopping is not an input: conditions 1–4 give it for every class outside the persistent core, which has at most one class, and no statement here assumes it for every model. The architecture is construction, then filtration, then counting: greatest refinements and heights (defined in `COMPANIONS.md`, "Further companion results") are companion results, not prerequisites of the count; the main proof permits a persistent core of at most one class; and global departure (every class eventually leaves the domains) is a separate statement, not used here: it is a conditional target of terminal refinement (`COMPANIONS.md`, "Further companion results"), to be proved from hypotheses of the count (the agreement of condition 3 and the nonempty losses of condition 4, with a Scott sentence for each class), not from its conclusions.

## Reduction to full presentations

This is the second route to the main theorem: it counts full presentations instead of expansion domains. Its generic layer is stated for an isomorphism-closed class \(K\) of countable structures in a countable relational base language \(L_0\); in the application \(L_0\) is the base-stage language of Layer 2 and \(K\) is the class of countable models of \(\Phi\). The organization is that of [AFK26], with the corrections recorded here and in `LITERATURE.md`, §9. Two comparisons are kept apart throughout:

- **exact comparison:** two full presentations for the same prescribed age are isomorphic; it counts the full presentations at a fixed level;
- **approximate comparison:** agreement of level observations at \(\eta\) gives back-and-forth equivalence at \(\eta\) of the base structures; it compares presentations of different levels and different ages.

Exact extension is needed at the level of full presentations, and approximation when their base reducts are compared. Level observations are a separate type from full templates: they do not inherit the uniqueness of full templates or exact extension.

**Structured geometry.** A **structured geometry** on a countable structure \(H\) is a family \(\mathcal C(H)\) of finite subsets of \(H\), its **closed** sets, closed under intersection and cofinal among the finite subsets, together with an **invariant** for every enumeration of a closed set. An invariant names a finite labelled diagram: which subsets of its points are closed, and the complete atomic diagram in the language considered, that is, the truth value of \(R(x_{s(0)},\dots,x_{s(k-1)})\) for every relation symbol \(R\) of every arity \(k\) and every map \(s\colon[k]\to[n]\), repetitions included (there is no restriction \(k\le n\)). The diagram is fixed by the invariant, uniformly across structures: the same invariant determines the same atomic facts in every model, not merely in each model separately. Within-model constancy is not enough: take an infinite set with every finite subset closed and one invariant \(q_n\) for all injective \(n\)-tuples, and add a unary base relation \(U\), empty in one structure and the whole set in another; every occurrence of \(q_n\) has the same diagram within each structure, and the two structures are not isomorphic. Restriction to closed subsets and change of enumeration agree with the prescribed diagrams. Every finite set has a least closed superset, its hull (intersect the closed sets containing it inside one of them), and the union of the hulls of the finite subsets of a set is a locally finite finitary closure operator whose finite closed sets are exactly \(\mathcal C(H)\) (Layer 0, "Finite closure"). The empty set and singletons need not be closed; that they are is a property of the application, stated where it is used.

**Fullness.** Let \(\mathcal A\) be a prescribed class of finite closed diagrams with specified **closed embeddings**. A structured geometry \(H\) is **full for** \(\mathcal A\) when its finite closed diagrams have age exactly \(\mathcal A\) and every pair of closed embeddings \(A\hookrightarrow H\), \(A\hookrightarrow B\) with \(A,B\in\mathcal A\) extends to a closed embedding \(B\hookrightarrow H\) over \(A\). In the **pointed** version a finite distinguished closed diagram is fixed, only closed diagrams containing it are considered, and all maps fix it.

**Exact comparison.** Two countable structures full for the same class \(\mathcal A\) are isomorphic over any common closed diagram; the same holds for pointed classes over the distinguished diagram. The proof alternates between the two structures, includes the next point in a finite closed extension, and realizes that extension over the current image by fullness; every finite tuple lies in one of the matched diagrams, so the union preserves every relation [BFKL14, Definition 1.4 and Theorem 1.5]. No amalgamation and no existence theorem is used: amalgamation enters only when a full structure is constructed. In the definitional expansion \(L^h_\lambda\) of Layer 2, closed embeddings are structure embeddings, and the statement is Mathlib's uniqueness of countable limits (`IsFraisseLimit.nonempty_equiv`, or extension pairs in both directions with `equiv_between_cg`) after a short lemma from fullness to extension pairs (ComputableModelTheory's rooted uniqueness, `exists_equiv_comp_eq_of_age_subset_of_countable` with `isExtensionPair_of_age_subset`, available at the pin, is that statement for an age given by representatives; its application to full structures is expected, not elaborated); in the relational stage chart language it is the exact-age comparison theorem of Layer 4.

**Full presentations and the count at one level.** For each \(\alpha<\omega_1\), a countable set \(I_\alpha\) indexes the **allowed full extension laws** at level \(\alpha\): finite ages \(\mathcal A_{\alpha,i}\), \(i\in I_\alpha\), with their closed embeddings, possibly over a named finite diagram. A **full presentation at level \(\alpha\)** is a countable structure full for one of these ages; forgetting its additional relations and named constants gives its **base reduct**, an \(L_0\)-structure. By exact comparison each index \(i\) gives at most one isomorphism type of base reducts, so a fixed level has countably many, and all countable levels together at most \(\aleph_1\). The index ranges over a countable family of allowed laws, not over arbitrary sets of finite invariants: the countable set of finite invariants has continuum many subsets, each a candidate age.

**Level observations.** For \(\eta<\omega_1\) and \(n<\omega\), let \(S^\eta_n\) be a countable set of observed invariants of \(n\)-tuples at level \(\eta\), and for \(\eta\le\xi\) let \(\tau^\xi_\eta\colon S^\xi_n\to S^\eta_n\) be projections with
\[
\tau^\eta_\eta=\mathrm{id},\qquad \tau^\xi_\eta\circ\tau^\zeta_\xi=\tau^\zeta_\eta\quad(\eta\le\xi\le\zeta),
\]
preserving arity and commuting with closed-face restriction, definedness included. A presentation \(H\) at level \(\alpha\) assigns to every closed tuple \(\bar a\) its **level observation** \(\operatorname{inv}^H_\eta(\bar a)\in S^\eta_n\) at each \(\eta\le\alpha\), coherently under the projections; observations are defined only there, on closed tuples and at levels at most \(\alpha\) (the empty tuple is observed only when the empty set is closed, and no level set need be inhabited otherwise); at level \(0\), equal observations give equal complete atomic \(L_0\)-diagrams, across structures. The level sets are separate sets with explicit projection maps. A single composition law \(\tau_\alpha\circ\tau_\beta=\tau_\alpha\) (\(\alpha\le\beta\)) of maps on one ambient set is not used: it does not give nested ranges (constant maps \(\tau_\alpha\equiv q_\alpha\) with distinct values satisfy it).

**Two-sided projections on one ambient set.** Suppose
\[
\tau_\alpha\circ\tau_\beta=\tau_\beta\circ\tau_\alpha=\tau_\alpha\quad(\alpha\le\beta).
\]
Each \(\tau_\alpha\) is idempotent, so its range equals its fixed-point set \(F_\alpha\), and \(F_\alpha\subseteq F_\beta\). Restricting \(\tau_\alpha\) to \(F_\beta\) gives coherent projections \(F_\beta\to F_\alpha\). If these sets are countable, they form a level-observation system.

Conversely, coherent projections between level sets \(S_\gamma\) define maps on their **tagged disjoint union**: \(\tau_\alpha\) projects a point at level \(\gamma>\alpha\) to level \(\alpha\), and fixes points at levels \(\gamma\le\alpha\). These maps satisfy the two-sided law, but their fixed-point sets are the cumulative unions \(\coprod_{\gamma\le\alpha}S_\gamma\), not the original \(S_\alpha\). Thus these constructions are not asserted to be inverse. The least fixing index of a tagged point is its tag; no identification with Scott rank, expansion stage, or height follows. For a general ambient system, some points may have no fixing index.

The application retains separate level sets and explicit projections. The common formal-top label has stage-dependent meaning; any single-ambient-set encoding must specify how stage types and that meaning are transported. These generic statements remain to be proved here.

Nonsurjectivity of the projections and first levels at which an invariant appears play no role; every element of \(S^\eta_n\) is an invariant at level \(\eta\). Written syntactically, the observations are predicates \(Q_{\eta,p}\) indexed by a level and an observed invariant, with \(Q_{\xi,p}(\bar x)\to Q_{\eta,\tau^\xi_\eta p}(\bar x)\): a closed tuple has one observation at each level, and exclusivity holds within a level, not between levels. The language of the levels below a countable ordinal is countable; the system over all of \(\omega_1\) is used semantically, and actual \(L_{\omega_1,\omega}\) theories are written only at countable levels (a disjunction over \(\aleph_1\) invariants is not a formula). Level observations are not the capped observations \(\operatorname{obs}_c\) of Layer 3; in the intended application, the observation at \(\eta\) of a closed tuple of a realization at \(\lambda_\alpha\) is the stage reduction of its chart type to \(\lambda_\eta\) (`StageType.reduce`, coherent by `StageType.reduce_reduce`).

**The approximate extension property (AE).** Let \(H\) and \(H'\) be presentations of levels at least \(\eta+1\), and let \(\bar a\) and \(\bar b\) enumerate closed sets of \(H\) and \(H'\) with \(\operatorname{inv}^H_{\eta+1}(\bar a)=\operatorname{inv}^{H'}_{\eta+1}(\bar b)\). Then for every finite closed extension \(\bar a\bar c\) in \(H\) there is a finite closed extension \(\bar b\bar d\) in \(H'\) of the same length with
\[
\operatorname{inv}^H_\eta(\bar a\bar c)=\operatorname{inv}^{H'}_\eta(\bar b\bar d),
\tag{AE}
\]
and symmetrically, with \(H\) and \(H'\) exchanged. Its exact form has four parts:

- the target tuple \(\bar b\) is kept literally, with its full invariant;
- only the two enlarged diagrams are compared, one level down;
- the matched extension lies in \(H'\), hence in the target's age, and respects the target's restrictions (top grades at most \(K\), the hollow condition, containment of a named core); this does not follow from coherence of the projections or from amalgamation at each level separately, exact fullness does not imply (AE) for two different ages, and (AE) does not assert that every permitted full invariant is realized exactly in every base reduct;
- a comparison starts from a specified match: the empty tuples, when the empty set is closed in both presentations and their observations agree, or a given matching of closed tuples; without it a family of matches may be empty, and then (AE) holds vacuously.

For pointed ages, (AE) is stated for all closed tuples used in the comparison; it is not inferred from fullness over the named diagram. At the finite level (AE) follows from a lifting statement, the **projected finite-extension rule**: given a source extension \(\bar a\bar c\) and a target root \(\bar b\) with the same observation at \(\eta+1\), the donor read from \(\bar a\bar c\) at level \(\eta\) over the observation of the root (the **projected donor**) lifts to a finite diagram allowed by the target's restrictions that retains the root exactly. That rule is a statement still to be proved, one case for each ordered pair of kinds of allowed ages (top-free, residual, hollow, rigid-core, and the persistent class if it has one). When the two presentations reduce to model expansions at \(\lambda_{\eta+1}\) (the reduction of a presentation to a model expansion at \(\lambda_{\eta+1}\) is the downward closure of Layer 5, a statement still to be proved), there is a second source of (AE) that needs no case analysis by kinds of ages: the finite-extension presentation of finite-cut receiving (Layer 3, "Receiving for finite extensions", compiled in this repository (`HasFiniteCutReceiving.hasFiniteExtensionReceiving`); it rests on (R1), which is still to be proved) of the target's expansion, at the cutoff \(\lambda_\eta\), with donor the type at \(\lambda_{\eta+1}\) of the source extension, which restricts literally to the common type of the two roots there. The source extension may add several points. Iterating one-point receiving retains each actual root literally, but a received occurrence agrees with its donor only below the cutoff, so the next one-point coface of the donor need not restrict literally to the newly received root. The finite-extension form repairs this at each step by the bounded-observation lifting of Layer 1: the next coface is replaced by a lawful coface of the received type with the donor's observation at an auxiliary cap, self-visible at the donor's arity and strictly between \(\lambda_\eta\) and \(\lambda_{\eta+1}\) (it exists because \(\lambda_{\eta+1}\) is a limit); the agreement at that cap is kept through the chain, and the observation at \(\lambda_\eta\) is read at the end. The received occurrence retains the target root literally, is realized in the target and hence lies in its age, and agrees with the donor below the cutoff. This is the one-block transfer of Layer 5 restricted to presentations.

**Approximate comparison.** If \(H\) and \(H'\) have levels at least \(\eta\), satisfy (AE) at every level below \(\eta\), and closed tuples \(\bar a\) and \(\bar b\) have equal observations at \(\eta\), then
\[
(H_0,\bar a)\equiv_\eta(H'_0,\bar b),
\]
back-and-forth equivalence at \(\eta\) of the base reducts (InfinitaryLogic's `BFEquiv`), and the same holds for any corresponding selection of coordinates, repetitions allowed. The proof is by induction on \(\eta\): at \(0\) it is the atomic condition; at a successor, include the requested point together with \(\bar a\) in one finite closed extension, apply (AE) and the induction hypothesis, and select the coordinates, and symmetrically for the back step; at a limit, it is the projection identities and the induction hypothesis. It is an application of InfinitaryLogic's `bfEquiv_of_gradedMatching` (available at the pin (signatures checked)), with the height guard and the selection of coordinates inside the relation (the match (i) of Layer 0) and the given closed tuples, with the given selection, as initial match. For abstract level observations and presentations, with (AE) and the atomic condition at \(0\) as hypotheses, it is `FullPresentation.bfEquiv_comp_of_obs_eq` (`Comparison/GradedMatchingApplications`), compiled in this repository (theorem named), which `Suggested.lean`, section 6, quotes; its instantiation to the presentations of the construction is not elaborated. Agreement on sentences of quantifier rank at most \(\eta\) then follows by `BFEquiv_implies_agreeQR`.

**The fundamental theorem.** Let \(K\) be an isomorphism-closed class of countable \(L_0\)-structures. Suppose that every member of \(K\) is isomorphic to the base reduct of a full presentation at some countable level, and that these presentations carry level observations satisfying (AE). Then:

1. \(|K/{\cong}|\le\aleph_1\);
2. at each \(\eta<\omega_1\), only countably many empty-tuple \(\equiv_\eta\)-classes occur in \(K\);
3. the codes on \(\mathbb N\) of members of \(K\) contain no perfect set of pairwise nonisomorphic structures;

and if \(K/{\cong}\) is uncountable, it has exactly \(\aleph_1\) elements. The first conclusion is the count at one level. For the second, fix \(\eta\): the base reducts with a full presentation below \(\eta\) have countably many isomorphism types; for every other type, choose a presentation at a level at least \(\eta\) and a closed tuple in it; its observation lies in the countable set \(\coprod_nS^\eta_n\), and two choices with the same observation give \(\eta\)-equivalent base reducts. For the third, analytic boundedness [MarDST, Corollary 5.16], applied to the forced back-and-forth trees of the pairs of distinct members of a hypothetical perfect antichain \(P\), gives one \(\eta<\omega_1\) at which distinct members of \(P\) are not \(\eta\)-equivalent, and the second conclusion makes \(P\) countable. In InfinitaryLogic this uniform separation is `exists_uniform_bfSeparation` (at our pinned dependency; Layer 0), composed there as `isThinOn_of_bfScattered` (`Descriptive/BFScattered`), which `VaughtConjecture.MainTheorem.Scatteredness` quotes as `isThinOn_of_countable_bfClasses` (Layer 6). No definability of \(K\) is used.

**Scatteredness and minimality.**

- Countably many observed types at every level give scatteredness (the second and third conclusions).
- One common starting observation (all presentations of level at least \(\eta\) have the same observation of the empty tuple, at every \(\eta\)) gives minimality: at each \(\eta\) all but countably many classes are \(\eta\)-equivalent, so every sentence has a countable truth side or a countable false side.

The fundamental theorem assumes only the first. The construction may have one common starting observation, but the generic theorem does not impose one.

**Three separate arguments.** Finite receiving gives back-and-forth comparison (the one-block transfer, or (AE) and approximate comparison); terminal classification gives the countable exceptions (the countable losses, or the count at one level); boundedness of analytic families of well-founded trees gives thinness. They are kept apart. A single cocountable back-and-forth class at every level is stronger than thinness needs, but it is what sentence minimality uses, and what makes an invariant Borel observation constant off a countable set; the interface below does not use the cocountable class itself, only that consequence. **Invariant Borel observations** (a prospective interface of InfinitaryLogic): an isomorphism-invariant Borel map on the codes of models on \(\mathbb N\), with values in a countably separated space, is determined by one countable back-and-forth level, that is, it is constant on the classes of \(\equiv_\alpha\) for some \(\alpha<\omega_1\); measurability is on codes only, never on the class quotient, and no sentence is recovered from the observation.

**Standard definitions** [Mon, §XII.1]. Here \(\equiv_\alpha\) and Scott rank are those of [Mon]: \(\equiv_\alpha\) is defined with moves by finite tuples [Mon, Definition II.32], and Scott rank is the parametrized one [Mon, Definition II.16]. A sentence is **scattered** when its countable models fall into countably many \(\equiv_\alpha\)-classes for every \(\alpha<\omega_1\) (equivalently, it is \(\Sigma^{\mathrm{in}}_\alpha\)-small for every \(\alpha\) [Mon, Definition XII.2]). A sentence is **bounded** when its countable models have Scott ranks bounded below \(\omega_1\), and **unbounded** otherwise [Mon, Definition XII.1]; a **counterexample** to Vaught's conjecture is a scattered unbounded sentence. A sentence \(\Theta\) is **minimally unbounded** [Mon, Definition XII.4] when it is unbounded, but for every sentence \(\psi\) one of \(\Theta\wedge\psi\) and \(\Theta\wedge\neg\psi\) is bounded. Lemma XII.8 and the rank-filtration comparison of `COMPANIONS.md` are stated in [Mon]'s convention; the offsets of [Mon, Lemmas XII.6 and XII.8] depend on it. Elsewhere this roadmap uses InfinitaryLogic's one-point `BFEquiv` and `internalScottRank`. The state of knowledge on the passage between conventions: InfinitaryLogic proves `BlockBFEquiv α → BFEquiv α` (`BlockBFEquiv.toBFEquiv`) and `BFEquiv (ω·α) → BlockBFEquiv α` (`BFEquiv.toBlock`), a multiplicative shift \(\alpha\mapsto\omega\cdot\alpha\), so the two agree at the limits \(\alpha\) closed under \(\beta\mapsto\omega\cdot\beta\), a closed unbounded set (a corollary not stated in the library and not needed here). The comparison with [Mon]'s \(\equiv_\alpha\), which is asymmetric and alternating and tests a finite vocabulary at level 0, is a statement still to be proved; its sketch goes through Karp's theorem [Mon, Theorem II.36] (\(\le_\alpha\) is inclusion of \(\Pi^{\mathrm{in}}_\alpha\)-types), the bound \(\omega\cdot\alpha\) on the quantifier rank of a \(\Pi^{\mathrm{in}}_\alpha\) formula, and [Mon, Observation II.35]. Likewise the comparison of [Mon]'s parametrized Scott rank with `internalScottRank` is still to be proved; the library has only `blockScottRank ≤ internalScottRank ≤ ω·blockScottRank`. A scattered sentence whose countable models have bounded Scott ranks has only countably many of them, so the countable models of \(\Phi\) must have unbounded Scott ranks. The bound "internal Scott rank at most \(\omega\)" (Layer 0; `COMPANIONS.md`, B3) is therefore a statement about the top-free witnesses in the full stage chart language only, not about the models of \(\Phi\). The countable truth sides of the main theorem imply the second clause of minimal unboundedness; for a scattered sentence such as \(\Phi\) the two are equivalent (they differ only for sentences that are not scattered), and unboundedness of \(\Phi\) is a separate statement (`COMPANIONS.md`, "Further companion results"), which also needs \(\Phi\) scattered: that follows from the absence of a perfect antichain by Silver's theorem on the Borel relations \(\equiv_\alpha\) [Mon, §XII.1], a statement still to be proved here.

**The least-level filtration.** Assume in addition that the empty set is closed in every presentation and that, at each \(\eta\), all presentations of level at least \(\eta\) have the same observation of the empty tuple. Let \(Q=K/{\cong}\), let \(Q_\alpha\) be the set of classes with a full presentation at level \(\alpha\), and put
\[
D_\eta=Q\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha<\eta}Q_\alpha .
\]
Then \(D_0=Q\), the \(D_\eta\) decrease and are continuous, their complements and their successor losses are countable, any two members of \(D_\eta\) are \(\eta\)-equivalent, and, if \(Q\) is uncountable, cofinally many successor losses are nonempty. Here \(D_\eta=\{q:r(q)\ge\eta\}\) for the least presentation level \(r(q)=\min\{\alpha:q\in Q_\alpha\}\), and continuity holds because \(r\) is a minimum. These are least presentation levels, not greatest refinement heights: no expansion is chosen canonically or proved maximal, and no existence theorem at limit stages is used. Nonempty losses at every level are not available in general, so this filtration is not an instance of the expansion-domain hypotheses; it enters the count as a filtration built from the rank \(r\).

**The lower-bound criterion.** Under the hypotheses of the fundamental theorem and the common starting observation, suppose that above every countable level there are two full presentations whose base reducts lie in \(K\) and are nonisomorphic. Then \(K/{\cong}\) is uncountable: otherwise each of countably many representatives has a Scott sentence [Mon, Theorem II.9] and a countable Scott isolating level [MarDST, Proposition 8.18], all bounded by one \(\gamma<\omega_1\), and two base reducts with presentations at levels at least \(\gamma\) would be \(\gamma\)-equivalent, hence isomorphic. Existence of presentations alone never gives the lower bound: that every auxiliary invariant occurs in some expansion does not give uncountably many base reducts, since one countable set can carry uncountably many different constant auxiliary labels without changing its base isomorphism type.

**Bounded levels** (a statement still to be proved; generic, using neither observations nor Scott sentences). Suppose that for every class \(q\) the set \(\{\alpha:q\in Q_\alpha\}\) of levels of its full presentations is bounded below \(\omega_1\), and that the levels \(\alpha\) with \(Q_\alpha\ne\emptyset\) are unbounded in \(\omega_1\). Then \(K/{\cong}\) is uncountable, because the supremum of countably many countable ordinals is countable. No countability of fibres is used. In `MainTheorem/Assembly` it would give `UncountablyManyClasses` from `FullPresentations` and the two hypotheses. The first hypothesis carries the content. Occurrence at unbounded levels is not enough by itself (the paragraph above). A bound for every class is a form of eventual departure for full presentations. In the application it is to come from expansion uniqueness and same-carrier transport, through the terminal stage of a class (Layer 6), or from eventual departure (`COMPANIONS.md`, "Further companion results", terminal refinement), which uses conditions 3 and 4 of the expansion-domain route. On that route condition 4 gives the lower bound directly, by disjoint losses, and this criterion is not used. The same argument applies to any relation between classes and countable ordinals in which a class may realize many values and a value may be realized by many classes (a statement still to be proved, with these hypotheses): if the values realized by each class are bounded below \(\omega_1\), and for every countable ordinal some class realizes a value strictly above it, then there are uncountably many classes. In the application the bound for each class is eventual departure (terminal refinement, item 1: conditions 3 and 4 with a Scott sentence for each class), applied to the levels of full presentations or to any invariant whose level is at most the stage of its presentation, and the unboundedness is supplied by the top-free witnesses. Neither uses global termination; full-presentation coverage still does ("The persistent core").

> **The four statements special to the construction** (statements still to be proved):
>
> 1. every base model admits an allowed full presentation;
> 2. at each level there are only countably many allowed full extension laws, not merely countably many invariants;
> 3. finite extensions remain compatible under observation: (AE), with the target's restrictions retained;
> 4. the base reducts do not collapse to countably many isomorphism types.
>
> Everything else is finite closed-set geometry, exact back-and-forth, bounded back-and-forth, or the final count.

In the application, the first is the terminal classification by exact ages of Layer 4 read in the other direction (each terminal model's expansion is full for its terminal condition's exact age: pointed at the named core in the rigid-core case, top grades at most \(K\) in the residual case, the hollow age in the hollow case), together with the top-free age (the section on the top-free witnesses, steps 1–6) and the persistent class (below); the second is the countable family \(\bigl(\sum_nS^\alpha_n\bigr)\sqcup\mathbb N\sqcup\{*\}\) of Layer 4 with the top-free age added (there \(S^\alpha_n\) is the set of stage types at \(\alpha\) on \(n\) points, the types of rigid cores, not the observation set \(S^\eta_n\) above, although in the instance the observations are stage types too); the third is the projected finite-extension rule; the fourth is the lower bound of Layer 6, from the top-free witnesses with expansion uniqueness and same-carrier transport, or from the criterion above. The immediate proof of the first uses the terminal-expansion theorem and hence the continuation and termination arguments of Layers 4–5: the full-presentation route is a legitimate alternative proof, not yet an elimination of those arguments.

**What the count of this route no longer uses, and what it still uses.**

- Expansion uniqueness and same-carrier transport are not used by the count (the countability of each \(Q_\alpha\), continuity, and agreement); besides the lower bound, they may enter the proof of the first special statement (through the terminal-expansion theorem). For the lower bound, on either route: on the route through losses they place the base class of a top-free witness in a loss; under the criterion above they separate the base reducts through terminality.
- The least presentation level is a total rank below \(\omega_1\), and a total rank gives eventual departure from the sets \(D_\eta=\{q:r(q)\ge\eta\}\). The simplification is that no separate termination theorem for expansion domains is needed, not that departure is absent.
- Scott separation on the persistent core is not used: the core of the least-level filtration is empty. Sentence separation (the thinness theorem for countable sentence splits) is still used for thinness in the minimality form; thinness in the scatteredness form (Layer 6) uses uniform back-and-forth separation instead.
- Of the hypotheses of the expansion-domain route, countable losses are replaced by the countability of each \(Q_\alpha\), limit continuity by nothing, logical agreement by approximate comparison, and nonempty losses at every level by the uncountability of \(Q\), which, for the least-level filtration (a total rank below \(\omega_1\) with countable fibres), is equivalent to cofinally many nonempty losses (InfinitaryLogic's `rankTail_cofinal_losses_iff` and `countable_iff_rank_bounded`, available at the pin (signatures checked); the latter is applied directly in the example of `Counting/Separation`); without such hypotheses the equivalence fails (`COMPANIONS.md`, terminal refinement, the lemma on cofinally nonempty losses).

**The persistent core.** The first special statement asks for a full presentation of every class, including a class with model expansions to every countable stage, the persistent core of the expansion-domain route, which that route bounds by one class and does not identify. Either a full presentation of the persistent class, if there is one, is constructed (for instance, if it is the class of the saturated model, reconstructed from the classical limit of the uncapped age of the section on the top-free witnesses), or the first statement is weakened to all but countably many classes. Under the weakening, the count, the filtration (the exceptions placed in \(D_0\setminus D_1\)), the truth sides, and thinness survive, but the exceptions must be shown countable by another argument, in practice condition 3 of the expansion-domain reduction. Global eventual stopping would also suffice; it is not assumed. Which of these holds is a statement still to be proved. The counting composition of this route is conditional on this coverage: the conditional theorems of `MainTheorem/Assembly` take as hypothesis `FullPresentations`, which asserts a full presentation of every class, a persistent one included. The known way to establish the coverage goes through global termination: every class leaves the expansion domains at a countable stage, and its terminal expansion there is full for its terminal exact age, which also empties the persistent core. The finite-stage arguments (the terminal classification by exact ages, the top-free age) do not cover the persistent class. So the full-presentation route currently depends on termination; the count of the expansion-domain route does not (it bounds the persistent core by one class, Layer 6). Eventual departure, the first half of global termination (its stopping half), is a conditional target of terminal refinement (`COMPANIONS.md`, "Further companion results"): it is to be proved by Scott isolation from the agreement on \(D_\beta\) (condition 3) and cofinally nonempty successor losses (given by condition 4), which are hypotheses of the expansion-domain count, not its conclusions (countable complements, the cardinality of \(Q\), and its uncountability are not used); under these hypotheses the persistent core is empty. The second half, that the terminal expansion is full for its terminal exact age, is the first special statement and is not part of terminal refinement. A full-presentation proof may therefore take departure from terminal refinement without circularity; it then depends on conditions 3 and 4 of the expansion-domain route (not on its count) and states that dependence. Departure proves neither terminal fullness (the first special statement) nor full-presentation coverage: a class that leaves the domains still needs a full presentation. A full-presentation proof that does not depend on conditions 3 and 4 of the expansion-domain route must cover every class, the persistent class included, by its own argument.

**Both routes are retained.** The expansion-domain route, whose count needs no global departure, and the full-presentation route, which may at first use the termination arguments of Layers 4–5 to prove its first special statement, are both kept. They are compared as complete verified proofs, not as hypothetical dependency diagrams. The order in which the second route is developed, and its Lean statements, are in `IMPLEMENTATION.md`, "The full-presentation route".

## The intended construction: the finite age and its classical limit

Condition 4 asks for a top-free terminal model at every countable block, the **top-free witness** of that block. It is to be obtained by constructing a finite age and reconstructing its classical limit. At the stage \(\lambda_\eta\), the finite top-free charts, read as finite structures in the definitional expansion of the stage chart language by the hull operations (Layer 2), form a class with the hereditary, joint embedding, and amalgamation properties. The classical Fraïssé existence theorem (available at the pin) gives a countable ultrahomogeneous structure whose age is that class. Reconstructing its partial evaluation from its chart relations gives a realization, and ultrahomogeneity, with the capped one-point cofaces in the age (the finite pinned extension of Layer 3 over a whole chart), gives its receiving property. The development proves the finite hypotheses and finite-age reconstruction; the general theorems (existence of the limit, extension of embeddings into an ultrahomogeneous structure, factorization of finite tuples through the age) are quoted from Mathlib and the two libraries of Layer 0. The seven steps, their order, and the acceptance criterion of finite-age reconstruction are in the section "The top-free witnesses: the finite age and its classical limit", after Layer 3.

**Two reconstruction problems.** The roadmap has two reconstruction problems, with different names and different acceptance conditions; the roadmap says which one it means (the steps of the section on the top-free witnesses, "reconstruction of partial evaluation" among them, are steps of the first). **Finite-age reconstruction** (the top-free witnesses, steps 3–7 of the section after Layer 3): build the classical Fraïssé limit of the finite age of top-free charts, recover from it a realization whose actual charts are read from the chart relations of the limit, and prove receiving and modelhood. It supplies models, the top-free witnesses of the lower bound. Its acceptance criterion is the one stated with its steps, item 11 of `SEMANTIC_CONTRACT.md`; the reconstruction roundtrip (`HULL_ALGEBRA.md`, §5) is part of it. **Higher-stage reconstruction** (Layers 4–5): recover from a base realization the canonical, partially defined tower of its expansions, its expansion to each stage \(\lambda_\xi\) to which it expands (unique, and defined on an initial segment of the stages, once outputs 4 and 5 of Layer 4 are proved). It explains the higher-stage structure of the models of \(\Phi\), and gives the normalization, uniqueness, and limit existence that the expansion domains use. Its outputs are the five listed at the head of Layer 4. Both are steps of the main theorem, and neither is a case of the other: finite-age reconstruction says nothing about the expansions of a model to later stages, and higher-stage reconstruction constructs no model from a finite age.

**Status.** This is the intended construction; this library contains its first three steps, two of them conditionally, steps 4 and 5 for a structure covered by top-free charts, and step 6 and the base-reduct part of step 7 for an ultrahomogeneous structure whose age is the age of top-free charts, the limit being a hypothesis. Steps 1–3 of the section on the top-free witnesses are compiled in this repository (theorem named): the finite top-free age and its hereditary closure (`hereditary_topFreeAge`, `ClassicalLimit/Age`, with no hypothesis), its amalgamation and joint embedding (`isFraisse_topFreeAge`, `ClassicalLimit/Amalgamation`), and the existence of its classical limit (`exists_isFraisseLimit_topFreeAge`), the last two conditional on the coatom extension property `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions` at the stage (the open part of (R6), Layer 3, 3.1, still to be proved) and on a countable stage that is a nonzero limit. Steps 4 and 5 are compiled in this repository (theorem named), in `ClassicalLimit/Reconstruction`, for a structure whose age is contained in the age of top-free charts: the reconstruction of partial evaluation with literal recovery of the chart relations (`reconstruct`, `reconstruct_eval_eq_some_iff`), exact consistency, covering, and top-freeness (`isConsistent_reconstruct`, `isCovering_reconstruct`, `isTopFree_of_reconstruct_eval`), and, for a structure whose age is the age of top-free charts, in particular a Fraïssé limit of it, a nonempty carrier and legal types as well (`reconstruct_of_age_eq`, `reconstruct_of_isFraisseLimit`); the limit itself is supplied by step 3, under the coatom extension property `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions`. Step 6 is compiled in this repository (theorem named), in `ClassicalLimit/Receiving`, for a structure whose age is the age of top-free charts and which is ultrahomogeneous, at a stage that is zero or a limit: the finite-cut receiving property of the reconstruction (`hasFiniteCutReceiving_reconstruct`), from the age equality, ultrahomogeneity, and capping, with no use of the coatom extension property or of modelhood. So is the base-reduct part of step 7: at a limit stage at least \(\omega\), the base-language structure of the reduction of the reconstruction to \(\omega\) satisfies the density sentence (`realize_densitySentence_reconstruct_reduce`). Both apply to the limit of step 3, whose existence needs `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions`. The hull operations are compiled for finite charts, and for exactly consistent covering realizations except closed images, the correspondence of substructures with charts, and preservation by chart-language embeddings whose source has a type that is not legal, the generation of the hull of a pair needing legality along that hull (Layer 2, "Status for realizations", for the hypotheses). The reconstruction roundtrip is compiled in direction (a) for every realization with legal types and in direction (b) for structures covered by top-free charts (`HULL_ALGEBRA.md`, §5). Direction (b) under local chart coverage by arbitrary charts, the rest of the classical route (modelhood at \(\lambda\), infinitude, and terminality, step 7), and the two-point bound on absolute indiscernibles (`COMPANIONS.md`, C) are statements still to be proved here, not accepted upstream dependencies. The coatom extension property and the rest of step 7 of finite-age reconstruction are statements still to be proved, the theorems of Layer 0 marked *available at the pin* are quoted, and those marked *available upstream, not yet at our pinned dependency* or *prospective* are quoted only once `IMPLEMENTATION.md`, "Dependency pins", records a pin containing them and their signatures are checked against it.

**What the classical theorem does not do.** The example as a whole is not an ordinary Fraïssé limit. Nor is a projected structure (a stage reduction of a model, or a presentation read at a lower level) thereby a Fraïssé limit of an age at the lower stage: exact extension within a specified age is to be proved for that age, and is not inherited by projection (the density boundary, Layer 3, 3.3). The classical theorem handles the top-free witnesses only: their charts carry proper and bottom labels, and receiving of a top-free donor there is exact. A model with top labels is not a limit of this age, and the classification needs the specialized finite decoding: stable continuation (Layer 4), exact residual receiving by LOW (statement (R2) of the table in Layer 3, 3.4), exact hollow-growth receiving (R3), the other terminal comparisons, and the proof that the terminal classes at a fixed stage are countable, all statements still to be proved. For a donor containing top labels, receiving, even in a top-free witness, is approximation at each permitted cutoff: for every cutoff \(\delta\) there is an extension serving the request at \(\delta\), different cutoffs may need different extensions, and no top is recovered. The expansion-domain counting argument of the reduction above is unchanged except for one step of Layer 6: the lower-bound step now reads "a terminal expansion supplies a loss only after uniqueness of expansion and same-carrier transport exclude another higher expansion of its base reduct", and eventual stopping is not an input (conditions 1–4 give it for every class outside the persistent core, which has at most one class); everything else in Layers 5–6 is unchanged. Classical countability of the limit supplies no computable enumeration of it.

**In short.** Build a class of finite closed configurations with amalgamation. Standard countable model theory assembles and compares the structures. The specialized finite decoding and the terminal classification produce an expansion filtration with countable exceptions. Generic counting and infinitary sentence separation finish the proof. The difficult mathematics is in three places: why these finite labelled configurations amalgamate (the coatom extension property, Layer 3, 3.1, (R6)), why their approximations support the required exact decoding (statements (R2)–(R4) of that table and their recovery statements), and why the terminal classes are countable (Layer 4).

### Layer 0 — General results with no construction imports

**Finite closure.** Develop finite closure operators and anti-exchange geometry, using standard notions. Prove that a finite hull operator has at most one finitary extension to all subsets, and that equality or equivariance on finite hulls extends to the global closure. The general result is reusable; the concrete global corollaries are optional for the main theorem.

**Capped extension.** For a restriction map between lawful section spaces and compatible observations, prove that lifting a prescribed face while preserving the ambient observation is equivalent to surjectivity from an observation fibre onto the corresponding face fibre. Do not assume injectivity, unrestricted caps, or that the observation itself is lawful.

**Directed finite observations.** Develop the bounded/eventually-constant versus unbounded/escaping dichotomy for monotone natural-valued functions on a directed preorder. Prove finite synchronization and appropriate cofinal-reindexing invariance. Reuse filters and \(\mathbb N_\infty\); do not introduce a new notion of infinitary convergence.

**Countable comparison.** Reuse the standard countable potential-isomorphism theorem. Prove only the specializations needed for rooted extendible families. Atomic compatibility of an arbitrary root is not enough: the root must belong to the selected extension family. The dense-set chain theorem (`Construction/DenseChain.lean`) belongs to the chain construction, which the main theorem does not use (the section on the top-free witnesses, after Layer 3).

The proof is an induction on \(\alpha\le h\) through `BFEquiv.zero`, `BFEquiv.succ`, and `BFEquiv.limit` (InfinitaryLogic, available at the pin). The height bound is explicit because the relations of the applications are defined only up to a level: the observations of a presentation up to its level, the reductions of types at \(\lambda_\eta\) up to \(\eta\). The initial match is a hypothesis, never a consequence of the other clauses: the empty relation satisfies all of them. Two applications are intended: approximate comparison of full presentations (the section "Reduction to full presentations"; initial match a given pair of closed tuples with equal observations), and the back-and-forth form of condition 3 of the expansion-domain reduction (initial match the common empty chart). Both applications are compiled through the generic form of the next paragraph, on abstract hypotheses; the statement above is kept as the specification of that special case, and no local proof of it is kept.

**The upstream graded-matching theorem and its two applications.** InfinitaryLogic's `bfEquiv_of_gradedMatching` (`Scott/GradedMatching`; available at the pin (signatures checked)), a generic form of the theorem above, takes a relation `R α n a b` defined at every ordinal `α`, with any language (no relationality), and guards its laws by the height: lowering (`β ≤ α → α ≤ height → R α n a b → R β n a b`), and forth and back from `R (α + 1)` into `R α` under `α + 1 ≤ height`; from `α ≤ height` and a related pair `R α n a b` (the initial match) it concludes `BFEquiv α n a b`. Because `R` is defined at every ordinal while the relations of both applications are defined only up to a level, each application puts the height guard \(\alpha\le\eta\) and the selection of coordinates inside `R`, with height \(\eta\). (i) *Approximate comparison* (the section "Reduction to full presentations"): `R α m a' b'` holds when there are a proof of \(\alpha\le\eta\) (the observations at \(\alpha\) take it), closed tuples \(\bar a\) and \(\bar b\) of one length \(n\) with equal observations at \(\alpha\), and a selection \(s\colon\mathrm{Fin}\,m\to\mathrm{Fin}\,n\) of coordinates, repetitions allowed, with \(\bar a\circ s=a'\) and \(\bar b\circ s=b'\); the initial match is the given pair of closed tuples, with the given selection \(s\). (ii) *Condition 3* (the reduction to expansion domains): a match at level \(\alpha\le\eta\) consists of model expansions of the two fixed base models to \(\lambda_\alpha\), a common chart (a stage type at \(\lambda_\alpha\) on \(k\) points), two covers (an actual occurrence of that chart in each expansion), and a selector \(\mathrm{Fin}\,n\to\mathrm{Fin}\,k\) through which the two tuples factor; the initial match is the common empty chart; lowering reduces both expansions and the chart, so no uniqueness of expansions is needed. The initial match of (ii) is two separate lemmas: existence (the empty set is closed in both expansions, so each has an actual occurrence of the empty chart) and compatibility (the two empty charts are equal, `StageType.eq_of_zero`). Both applications are compiled through the upstream theorem, on abstract hypotheses: `FullPresentation.bfEquiv_comp_of_obs_eq` and `ExpansionMatchData.bfEquiv_of_expansionMatch` (`Comparison/GradedMatchingApplications`), each compiled in this repository (theorem named); their instantiation to the construction (the presentations of the full-presentation route for (i), the expansion domains of Layer 5 for (ii)) is not elaborated. - **Permitted cutoffs and self-visible caps** are different notions and keep different names. A **permitted cutoff** at stage \(\alpha\) is a label \(c\) with \(\bot<c<\alpha\); ordinal zero is one. Receiving and the density sentence quantify over permitted cutoffs. - A **root** is an actual occurrence of \(R\): a literal injective tuple \(t\) of some length \(n\ge0\) together with its evaluated type \(p\). The **empty root** (\(n=0\)) is the base case of every statement below; a statement that needs a root of positive length says so and treats the empty root separately (3.4).
- A **donor type** over the root is a stage type \(d\) with a face embedding \(f\) of the root's coordinates into those of \(d\) along which \(d\) restricts literally to \(p\); in the table of 3.4 it is a **one-point coface** of \(p\), a stage type on \(n+1\) points whose restriction to the first \(n\) is \(p\), unless stated otherwise. Receiving at a cutoff is weaker than exact receiving, and it never yields equality at the formal top (3.3). Two statements of this property are kept apart. For the top-free witnesses it is compiled in this repository (theorem named), for the reconstruction of an ultrahomogeneous structure whose age is the age of top-free charts (`hasFiniteCutReceiving_reconstruct`, step 6 of the section on the top-free witnesses), from the age equality, ultrahomogeneity, and capping, with no model clause; such a structure, the classical limit, exists under `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions` (step 3). For every model it is (R1) of the table in 3.4, still to be proved; condition 3 of the reduction to expansion domains (through the one-block transfer of Layer 5), the direction from the four-family sentence to the density sentence at stage \(\omega\) (the fidelity theorem of Layer 2), and (R2), whose LOW construction takes its occurrence from (R1), need it. The first does not give the second: a model, or a model expansion, need not be a Fraïssé limit. The property descends along stage reduction, one permitted cutoff at a time, between stages that are zero or limits (`HasFiniteCutReceiving.reduce`, `Realization/Receiving`, compiled in this repository (theorem named)); this is not exact projected receiving (3.3).
- **Receiving for finite extensions.** An all-finite-extension presentation of the finite-cut receiving property, compiled in this repository (`HasFiniteCutReceiving.hasFiniteExtensionReceiving`, `Realization/Receiving`): for every root, every donor type over it with any finite number of new points, and every permitted cutoff, there is an extension of the root by fresh points that is one actual occurrence with the donor's observation at the cutoff. It is equivalent to the one-point presentation (`hasFiniteExtensionReceiving_iff`). The many-point form implies the one-point form at once. The converse proceeds one point at a time along a chain of visible faces of the donor's plan at an auxiliary cap. The bounded-observation lifting of Layer 1 is available at caps self-visible at the target grade, not at every permitted cutoff, so for a requested cutoff \(c\) the chain is run at an auxiliary ordinal \(c'\) chosen strictly between the requested cutoff and the stage, \(c<c'<\alpha\) (a permitted cutoff, so never the formal top), self-visible at the donor's arity \(k\). Such a \(c'\) is given by `Label.exists_lt_lt_isSelfVisible (hβ : Order.IsSuccPrelimit β) (ho : o < β) (k : ℕ) : ∃ c : Ordinal, o < c ∧ c < β ∧ IsSelfVisible k (c : Label)` (`Label/Visibility`, namespace `Label`), applied with \(o:=c\), with witness \(c'=c+(k+1)\). Its hypotheses hold here only because (i) the requested cutoff \(c\) is an ordinal below the stage (it is a permitted cutoff, `isPermittedCutoff_coe`), and (ii) the stage is zero or a limit (`Order.IsSuccPrelimit`). Each step takes a lawful coface of the actual type with the donor's observation at \(c'\); this stronger agreement at \(c'\) is preserved throughout the finite chain of extensions, after which one passes down to \(c\) by capping. The stage hypothesis (ii) stays in the statement. At stage zero there are no permitted cutoffs, so that case is vacuous; it is not a source of a witness. No generalization to successor stages follows from this argument. The hypotheses of the converse are exact consistency of the realization and (ii); no countability is used, so it covers stage \(\omega\), where the cutoffs are natural numbers, and the stages \(\lambda_\xi\) (all limits). The quantifier order is kept: for each donor and each permitted cutoff there exists an extension, and different cutoffs may use different extensions.
- The **private context** of a receiving construction is an actual occurrence of \(R\) containing the root as a literal face. It is acquired in the model by uniformity, high-arity dominance, and generalized saturation ([Kni26, Definition 3.2.1], clauses 4(b), 4(c), and 4(a)i), as in [Kni26, Lemma 8.1.1], clauses 3 and 4, with its threshold above a requested floor; the proof of that lemma cites high-arity dominance as clause 4(a) of Definition 3.2.1, where it is clause 4(c). Its type carries the cells the construction reads: the **private cap** \(C\), a cell of full scope whose grade \(N\), the **threshold**, exceeds the arity of the donor; the **marker**, a cell below \(C\); and the **reference cells**, cells below \(C\), one for each block \([\mu,\mu+\omega)\) (\(\mu\) zero or a limit) that contains a proper donor label, labelled \(\mu+k\) with \(k<N\) (in (R4) this is the stable value, and the reference of the block \(\alpha\) is the marker itself). Its **actual cut** is the largest of the ordinals \(\mu+N\) over these blocks; in (R1) the block of the requested cutoff is also requested, so that the actual cut lies above the cutoff. In (R1) the reference cells are the **anchors** of the row of the gate: a proper donor label \(\mu+i\) outside the root, with finite part \(i\le N\), is read by that row as the visibility replacement \(\operatorname{vr}_N(\cdot,i)\) of its entry at a private cell of the block \(\mu\) whose label has finite part strictly below \(N\) (so is not self-visible at \(N\)) and is at most the label of the private cap (the anchor of that label); a root or private cell already in the block serves as one. The marker is not used in (R1); it is read by `Correct` (3.3). The gate (below) is a cell of the constructed scheme, not of the private context.
- The **master chart** of a construction is the given finite chart that it extends: the private context, the chart of a root in the classical limit (R5), or, in the retained chain construction, the master chart of a condition. Every row, occurrence, and label of the master chart is retained literally.
- **Padding** enlarges the arity of the constructed chart by coordinates that carry prescribed labels and no request. **Freshness**: designated coordinates are new points, every cell whose support meets one of them is a new cell of the construction, and new cells are not identified with old cells merely because their roles or observations agree.
- The **bottom pattern** of a labelling records which cells are labelled bottom, as in the bottom-pattern family of [Kni26, Definition 3.2.1], clause 4(a)ii. That clause tests the pattern only on the cells of grade at most \(n\), where \(n\) is the arity of the occurrence being extended (the set \(D^{\le n}\) of the clause); a cell that a construction needs to be non-bottom on the realized occurrence (the gate of (R1)) must have grade at most \(n\). A **literal-top prescription** requires given cells to carry the formal top itself, not a large proper value.
- A labelling is **orderly** when each value is self-visible at the grade of its cell [Kni26, Definition 2.3.4]. The rows \(E(\Sigma)\) of a scheme are orderly [Kni26, Definition 2.5.3], and a labelling is **lawful** when it is orderly and respects the rows [Kni26, Definition 2.5.4]: **locality**, the row \(E(\Sigma)\) transforms in the sense of [Kni26, Definition 2.3.9] to the labelling of the cells below \(\Sigma\) capped at its value at \(\Sigma\); and **availability**, clause 2 there.
- A **legal scheme** is a domain in the sense of [Kni26, Definition 2.6.1]: a scheme whose rows are consistent [Kni26, Definition 2.5.12], bountiful [Kni26, Definition 2.5.14], and complete [Kni26, Definition 2.5.15] (every graded face carries a cell; completeness is defined for bountiful rows), and coded. **Coding** is the range normalization of the rows: every row entry is bottom or an ordinal \(\omega\cdot i+j\) with \(i,j\) natural numbers, that is, an ordinal below \(\omega^2\). Legality imposes it as a condition, checked on the rows each construction builds; it is not derived from [Kni26, Lemma 2.5.13], whose offset bound is not correct as stated (Layer 1). A construction that also needs the bound \(j\le g+1\) on the entries of a row of grade \(g\) imposes that bound on its constructed rows; it is not part of legality. The recoverability that Definition 2.6.1 also asks for, that each cell determines its graded face and the plan, cells, and rows below it, holds by representation: the rows are part of the data of a scheme. In Lean legality is the predicate `Scheme.IsLegal`, whose coding clause `Scheme.IsCoded` is exactly the range normalization. A **legal stage type** is a stage type on a legal scheme; the relation symbols of Layer 2 are indexed by legal stage types.
- The **fields** of a construction are the cells of the private scheme and of the donor scheme, together with one cutoff field in the LOW construction; a **state vector** assigns a label to each field. Each construction defines when a vector is **admitted** at grade \(j\): its private and donor parts are lawful on the cells of grade at most \(j\), they agree on the root, every field of grade greater than \(j\) is self-visible at grade 1, and the construction's own condition holds (`Correct` in the growth construction of (R3) and (R4), once \(j\ge N\) and the private part lies in the prescribed bottom class of 3.2; the LOW forcing in the LOW construction, 3.3). The **catalogue** at grade \(j\) is the finite set of canonical admitted vectors at grade \(j\): normal forms, without top, whose values lie in a finite set fixed by \(j\) and the number of fields. Every admitted vector at grade \(j\) is the image of a catalogue vector under a **decoder**, a witness bounded by grade \(j\) (3.1) that sends a catalogue value to bottom only if it is bottom. The **future fields** at grade \(j\) are the fields of grade greater than \(j\); every replacement of an admitted vector at grade \(j\) retains them literally.
- **Owners**, **short** and **long-row** owners, **ownerwise decoding**, **long-row locality**, and the **section theorem** are defined in 3.1.
- The vocabulary of checkpoint 2.4 (3.1, (R6)). The **grade cut** of a scheme at a grade \(g\) consists of its cells of grade at most \(g\), with their rows read literally. A **source prefix** of a scheme below a graded face \(Z\) is a lower embedding of a smaller scheme (its source) over the same points, keeping scopes, whose image contains every cell below \(Z\). The **splice** of two labellings at a grade \(j\) is the first on the cells of grade at most \(j\) and the second above; it is a different operation from the splice of two witnesses (3.1, the transformation lemma). The **lift at the lower grade** of a lift from \((C,j+1)\) to \((B,j+1)\) is the capped lift from \((C,j)\) to \((B,j)\). The **owner of a prescription** below a graded face \(X\) is an owner of graded index \(X\) whose label is the largest among the prescribed cells of the grade of \(X\). An **owner-capped lift** is a lawful labelling below the target pair that reads the prescription capped at the owner label below the source pair and keeps the observation of the ambient labelling at the cap at every cell. A **lawful companion** of a labelling \(\nu\circ r\) is a lawful labelling bottom exactly where \(\nu\circ r\) is; for \(r\) lawful and \(\nu\) a witness bounded by a grade bounding the cells, \(\nu\circ r\) is lawful exactly when it has a lawful companion, so its bottom pattern is the only obstruction. The **flattened source** of a labelling with values in a finite set \(V\), at a grade \(m\), is the flattening of finite parts at \(m\) of its strongly coded normal form at \(m\); it is the **coded source section** of the owner alignment, lawful, short at \(m\), and never the formal top. For the alignment of owners, fix an owner \(o\) of grade \(m\), maximal among the cells below it, a lawful prescription \(p\) below \(o\), and a cap \(\gamma\) self-visible at \(m\) with \(\bot<\gamma<p(o)\). A **source** below \(o\) is a lawful labelling \(s\) of the cells below \(o\), short at \(m\) (every finite part at most \(m\)), with \(s(o)\ne\top\), together with a witness \(\tau\) bounded by grade \(m\) with \(\tau\le\gamma\) and \(\tau\circ s=\min(p,\gamma)\); in the one-grade step it is the row of the serving cell (below). A cell \(e\) is **saturated** when \(\tau(s(e))=\gamma\). The **strip** of a label \(\omega b+i\) at grade \(m\) is the band \([\omega b,\omega b+m]\) at the start of its block. The **source cap** \(h\) is the least value of \(\operatorname{vr}_m(s(e),m)\) over the saturated cells. The owner-local alignment gives a **reading cap** \(\delta\), self-visible at \(m\) with \(\gamma\le\delta\le p(o)\), and a witness \(\rho\) bounded by grade \(m\), the **alignment decoder**, with \(\rho(h)=\delta\), \(\min(p(e),\delta)=\min(\rho(s(e)),\delta)\) at every cell, and \(h\le s(e)\) at every cell with \(\delta<\min(p(e),p(o))\); when some cell with \(\gamma<\min(p(e),p(o))\) has its source below \(h\), the alignment decoder reads the strip of \(h\) through the capped witness of the locality of \(p\) after the **block shift**, the translation of that strip onto the strip that the locality of \(s\) carries onto it. For \(\mu\) zero or a limit above \(h\) and a finite set \(V\) of labels, the **tail code** of a label \(y\) is \(\mu+\operatorname{strongEncode}_{V,m}(y)\), the translation by \(\mu\) of a strongly coded code, which need not be strongly coded itself; the **tail decoder** is bottom below \(\mu\) and decodes the rest after removing \(\mu\); the **high code** of \(y\) is bottom when \(y\le\delta\) and its tail code otherwise. The **aligned encoding** at a cell \(d\) is \(\max(\min(s(d),h),\text{the high code of }\min(p(d),p(o)))\), and the **extended decoder** \(x\mapsto\max(\min(\rho(x),\delta),\text{the tail decoder at }x)\) reads it literally as \(\min(p,p(o))\). In the one-grade step from \((C,j+1)\) to \((B,j+1)\), the **serving cell** of a lift is a cell of graded index \((B,j+1)\) at which the ambient labelling reaches the cap (availability), and its row is the source. The **lift at an ambient** \(S\) (`CappedLiftAt`) is the capped lift whose ambient is \(S\), at positive caps and for prescriptions never the formal top. The **boundary** is the set of cells below \((B,j+1)\) that lie below one of the two coatoms \(U\), \(V\) at grade \(j+1\), the cells of the amalgam; the rows **extend from the boundary** at a cap \(h\) along \(S\) (`ExtendsFromBoundary`) when every labelling lawful below \(U\) and below \(V\) that agrees with \(S\) capped at \(h\) on the boundary extends, unchanged there, to a labelling lawful below \((B,j+1)\) that agrees with \(S\) capped at \(h\) everywhere.

#### 3.1. Statements of the finite extension constructions, and the section theorem

For each finite extension construction, state separately:

- **input data:** the master chart and the root in it, the donor type with its face embedding, the permitted cutoff, the padding length, the designated fresh coordinates, the bottom pattern, the literal-top prescriptions, and the cells of the private context that the construction reads (private cap, marker, reference cells);
- **compatibility conditions:** the donor type restricts literally to the root's type; the private context contains the root as a literal face, and the two restrictions to the root agree cell by cell; the threshold exceeds the donor's arity and every requested offset; the bottom pattern and the literal-top prescriptions are consistent with the donor type;
- **constructed finite object:** a legal scheme (plan, cells, grades, rows) with embeddings of the master chart and of the donor scheme and, where the statement uses one, a display; all of these are data, not propositions asserting existence;
- **literal equations:** the private type and the donor type are the literal ordered face-map restrictions of the constructed scheme: its restriction along the private embedding is the master chart's scheme and its restriction along the donor embedding is the donor's scheme, with the same cells in the same order and the same rows, not merely the same lawful labellings; the root is their literal common face; the display restricts literally to the private labels and to the donor labels, and has the prescribed bottom pattern and literal tops; the designated fresh coordinates are new.

Each statement is proved:

1. for every root size, including the empty root, and every padding length, including zero; where a construction needs a root of positive length, its statement in 3.4 gives the base case of the empty root;
2. with designated freshness and literal retention of the root and of every row, occurrence, and label of the master chart;
3. with bottom patterns and literal-top prescriptions given separately: neither is derived from the other, and realizing a bottom pattern does not realize literal tops;
4. with legality of the constructed scheme, its bountifulness proved at every self-visible cap of the target grade, each lift preserving the observation at its cap on every cell of the target, including the auxiliary cells the construction introduces and the future fields, even where the recovery statement that uses the construction ignores them; and with the future fields retained literally by every replacement of an admitted vector.

**Per-cap lifting, not simultaneous preservation.** Per-cap lifting is bountifulness [Kni26, Definition 2.5.14]: for graded faces \(X\preceq Y\) of the scheme, with \(j\) the grade of \(Y\), every cap \(c\) self-visible at \(j\) (clause 7: \(c=\operatorname{vr}_j(c,j)\)), and every lawful labelling \(q\) of the cells below \(Y\), every lawful labelling \(p\) of the cells below \(X\) with \(\operatorname{obs}_c(p)=\operatorname{obs}_c(q|_X)\) extends to a lawful labelling \(q'\) below \(Y\) with \(q'|_X=p\) literally and \(\operatorname{obs}_c(q')=\operatorname{obs}_c(q)\). At a cap that is not self-visible at \(j\) the lift is not available in general: [Kni26, §2.5] notes that \(q\) may then provide extra information about \(p\). Simultaneous preservation, one extension of \(p\) with \(\operatorname{obs}_c(q')=\operatorname{obs}_c(q)\) at every compatible self-visible cap \(c\), is not proved and is not needed. It does not follow from per-cap lifting through a largest compatible cap. When \(p\) and \(q|_X\) differ, let \(m\) be the least value of \(\min(p(x),q(x))\) over the cells \(x\) where they differ; the compatible caps are the caps self-visible at \(j\) and at most \(m\), and they need not have a maximum. At target grade 3 with \(m=\omega\cdot2+1\) they are bottom and the ordinals \(i\) and \(\omega+i\) with \(3\le i<\omega\); with \(p(x)=\omega+1\) and \(q(x)=\omega+2\) they are bottom and the natural numbers \(i\ge3\). When the compatible caps do have a maximum, a lift at it preserves every compatible cap, because caps nest under \(\min\); when \(p=q|_X\), \(q\) itself preserves every cap. The only known route to simultaneous preservation in general is through injectivity of restriction (uniqueness of extension), which is not available (Layer 0). The main theorem needs only per-cutoff extension: density is \(\forall\) root, \(\forall\) donor, \(\forall\) cutoff, \(\exists\) extension, and different cutoffs may use different extensions.

**The section theorem and the shared decoding lemma.** Ownerwise decoding is an explicit target, proved once. It belongs to the constructions: it supplies lawful sections of the constructed schemes in the bountifulness proofs of this item, and it is not a recovery statement.

A **witness** in the sense of [Kni26, Definition 2.3.9] is a pair \(\langle g,\sigma\rangle\) of a **suppressor** \(g\) (a non-increasing function from grades to labels, each value self-visible at its grade) and a **shifter** \(\sigma\) (a map on labels) satisfying clauses 3–5 there. Let \(g_K\) be the suppressor that is top at the grades at most \(K\) and bottom above. A value map \(\nu\) on labels is a **witness bounded by grade \(K\)** when \(\langle g_K,\nu\rangle\) is a witness. Spelled out: \(\nu(\bot)=\bot\); \(\nu\) is monotone; \(\nu(\operatorname{vr}_k(x,i))=\operatorname{vr}_k(\nu(x),i)\) for every label \(x\) and all \(i\le k\le K\); and, for every \(k>K\), if \(\nu(x)=\bot\) then \(\nu(\operatorname{vr}_k(x,i))=\bot\) for all \(i\le k\). The last condition is clause 5 of the definition at the grades \(k>K\), where it applies whenever \(\nu(x)\le g_K(k)=\bot\), and it does not follow from the others: for \(K=1\), the map that sends \(0\) and \(1\) to bottom and fixes every other label satisfies the first three, but \(\nu(\operatorname{vr}_2(0,2))=\nu(2)=2\neq\bot\). A label is **short** at grade \(g\) when it is bottom, top, or an ordinal whose finite part is at most \(g\). An **owner** is a cell together with its row; it is a **short owner** when every entry of its row is short at its grade, and a **long-row owner** otherwise.

**Statement.** Let \(r\) be a lawful labelling (the input) of the cells of a scheme below a graded face whose cells all have grade at most \(K\), and let \(\nu\) be a witness bounded by grade \(K\), applied cellwise. Suppose that every owner \(c\) is short or satisfies the **mapped locality** \(E(c)\Rightarrow\bigl(d\mapsto\min(\nu(r(d)),\nu(r(c)))\bigr)\) on the cells \(d\) below \(c\). Then \(\nu\circ r\) is lawful. Orderliness and availability are transported through \(\nu\) once, for all cells. Locality is proved owner by owner: at a short owner by composing the input's locality with \(\nu\), which is possible because short entries are unchanged by flattening of finite parts at the owner's grade; at any other owner it is the hypothesis. The lemma assumes neither lawfulness of the decoded section, nor bottom reflection of \(\nu\), nor shortness of the inherited rows. Shortness is not a consequence of legality: coding bounds the entries only below \(\omega^2\), and even the bound \(j\le g+1\), where a construction imposes it, allows an entry whose finite part exceeds the grade by one. The new rows of a construction are short by their support, not by their coding.

**The section theorem.** In a construction with fields and a catalogue, let \(S\) be admitted at grade \(j\), and let \(a\) be a catalogue vector and \(\nu\) a decoder with \(\nu\circ a=S\). The construction defines from \(a\) a lawful **source section** \(r_a\) of the constructed scheme below grade \(j\). Its owners are of two kinds. The new rows, of full scope and grade at least 2, are short, by their support. The inherited cells carry the rows of the original schemes literally, and \(r_a\) is determined on them cell by cell by the values of \(a\) (the **exact base table**); their rows may be long. For an inherited owner, **long-row locality** is its mapped locality, obtained by transporting the lawfulness of \(\nu\circ a=S\) on the original rows along the exact base table; no bottom reflection of the decoder is used. The lemma then makes \(\nu\circ r_a\) a lawful section of the constructed scheme whose values on the original cells are those of \(S\). This is the **section theorem** of the construction, the step of the bountifulness proof that realizes an admitted vector by a section; **ownerwise decoding** is its owner-by-owner passage from the lawfulness of \(r_a\) to that of \(\nu\circ r_a\).

Both the growth construction (the legal scheme shared by (R3) and (R4) of 3.4) and the LOW construction (R2) are required applications: each is shown to satisfy the hypothesis owner by owner, its new owners by shortness and each inherited long row by long-row locality. The lemma shares the owner-by-owner argument; it does not identify the rows of the two constructions.

**A transformation lemma still to be proved in this layer.** The band rule of Layer 1 (a witness bounded by grade \(K\), followed by the band map that sends the formal top into the band \([\alpha,\alpha+K]\)) covers only the top-witness row of the proof of [Kni26, Lemma 5.3.5]: the row \(E(\Theta)\) of a full-scope cell \(\Theta\) of grade \(K\) labelled top, the **top-witness cell**, whose witness that proof composes with the band map. The row \(E(\Sigma)\) of any other top-labelled cell \(\Sigma\), of grade \(J\le K\) and below \(\Theta\), needs a **splice of two witnesses** with the same source \(E(\Sigma)\): a witness \(E(\Sigma)\Rightarrow q\), where \(q\) is the labelling of the cells below \(\Sigma\) (the locality of the labels at \(\Sigma\); its suppressor is top at the grades up to \(J\), and the values of \(q\) are below \(\alpha\) or top), and a witness \(E(\Sigma)\Rightarrow r\), where \(r(d)=\min(E(\Theta)(d),E(\Theta)(\Sigma))\) for the cells \(d\) below \(\Sigma\), the row of \(\Theta\) on those cells capped at its entry at \(\Sigma\) (a witness given by the consistency of the rows). The spliced target is \(q\) at the cells where \(q\) is below \(\alpha\) and, at the cells where \(q\) is top, the band map of \(r\) with a base \(\lambda\) (zero or a limit) that \(r\) does not fall below on those cells, and the splice asserts \(E(\Sigma)\Rightarrow\) spliced target, on cells of grade at most \(J\). It is not a general composite: each of the two regions carries its own hypotheses. The printed proof of Lemma 5.3.5 obtains these rows through the transitivity of Lemma 2.3.14, which is not correct as stated (Layer 1); the splice replaces that step. It is a statement still to be proved here; the lawfulness of the stable candidate in Layer 4 uses it.

##### (R6): the coatom extension property and the completion of the amalgam

**What is established.** Let \(A\) be a set of \(m+2\) points with distinct points \(a,b\), and let \(t_a\) and \(t_b\) be legal stage types on the coatoms \(A\setminus\{a\}\) and \(A\setminus\{b\}\) that agree literally on their common face \(A\setminus\{a,b\}\) (a closed face of both). Their **amalgam** [Kni26, Definition 4.3.1] is constructed as data: its cells and rows are those of the two schemes, the cells of the common face counted once, and its labelling is the union of the two labellings. Its rows are consistent and bountiful [Kni26, Lemma 4.3.2], and its restrictions to the two coatoms are literally \(t_a\) and \(t_b\), with the same cells in the same order, rows and labels included. It is not complete: every graded face whose scope is not \(A\) carries a cell, but no cell has scope \(A\), so the amalgam is not a legal scheme. Its cells, with their rows and labels, are the **boundary** of its completion. The exact pinned extension reduces to one statement about stage types:

- The **coatom extension property** at a stage \(\alpha\): any two legal stage types at \(\alpha\) on the two coatoms of a set of \(m+2\) points that agree literally on their common face (a closed face of both) are the literal faces, labels included, of one legal stage type on the \(m+2\) points. Its **apex form** asks in addition for a cell of full scope and full grade \(m+2\) that carries the maximum of the labels, as in [Kni26, Corollary 4.3.22]; the apex is what high-arity dominance uses [Kni26, Lemma 4.4.3], while the nonemptiness of the uniformity instances [Kni26, Lemma 4.4.2] uses the plain form. The plain form follows from the apex form by forgetting the apex. In Lean the two forms are the hypotheses `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions` and `StageType.HasApexCoatomExtensions`.

From the plain form follow the exact pinned extension (statement (R6): one coatom extension for each point of the chart outside the face, each point chosen so that the face enlarged by it is closed), the one-point extension of every legal stage type [Kni26, Proposition 4.3.23], and the amalgamation of two legal stage types over a common face. The last, capped at a proper cap self-visible at the arity of the amalgam and above every label of the two charts, is the amalgamation of top-free charts (step 2 of the classical limit, in the section on the top-free witnesses; `StageType.exists_isTopFree_amalgam`, compiled in this repository (theorem named), conditional on `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions` at a nonzero limit stage); (R6), capped in the same way, is (R5), which, used only over a whole chart (where no coatom extension is needed), gives receiving in the classical limit (step 6 there). [Kni26, Proposition 4.3.24], for the domains that are legal schemes, needs no hypothesis: the bottom labelling of a legal scheme is a legal stage type at every stage. The exact pinned extension uses nothing about the stage; the stage enters only in the proof of the coatom extension property. What remains for (R6) is that proof, in the apex form: the completion of the amalgam by cells of full scope.

**The naive completion.** The completion of [Kni26, Definition 4.3.14] has cells of full scope indexed by all lawful coded patterns (the efficient stacks of Definitions 4.3.3, 4.3.5, 4.3.6, and 4.3.7), with the rows between full-scope cells of the same grade given by the heights of their meets (clause 3 there). The construction chosen instead builds the completion grade by grade over the boundary, in one fixed order of its cells; it chooses the full-scope cell that serves each lift (defined with 2.6) before extending on the other coatom, and it tops out in a single apex cell.

#### 3.2. One occurrence, then labelled evaluation

Keep three steps separate:

- **Construction:** the finite object of 3.1, as data, with its lifting properties at every self-visible cap and the conditions on every auxiliary row it introduces.
- **Extension of the realization by one occurrence** over the private context, by a model clause that depends on the statement:
  - *(R1), the ordinary construction.* The display shows that the parameter of the bottom-pattern clause 4(a)ii of [Kni26, Definition 3.2.1] is admissible. Applied over the private tuple, the clause gives an actual one-point extension \(u\) on the constructed scheme with the display's bottom pattern on the cells of grade at most the private arity. The graded index of the gate has grade \(N\), at most that arity, so each of its cells is tested: the gate is not bottom in \(u\) (the gate equation), and its twins are bottom in \(u\). The recovery statement reads the display only through this bottom pattern, and not its values.
  - *(R2), the LOW construction.* The occurrence comes from (R1): finite-cut receiving over the private context, in its form for finite covers, with the display on the constructed LOW scheme as donor type and a cutoff \(\delta\) above the rounded non-top donor maximum \(\beta_0\) of 3.3. Its recovery statement reads the observations of the received occurrence below \(\delta\) and compares them with those of the display.
  - *(R3) and (R4), the growth construction.* Generalized saturation, clause 4(a)i, realizes the constructed legal scheme over the private tuple: an actual one-point extension \(u\) on exactly that scheme. No display, no bottom pattern, and no stage bound for a display is used.

  In each case, exact consistency makes the restriction of \(u\) to the private tuple the private context, labels included, and the restriction of \(u\) along the donor embedding (the root followed by the new point) an actual occurrence on the donor's scheme whose labels are those of the corresponding cells of \(u\); the new point is fresh over the whole private tuple, hence over the root.
- **Labelled evaluation.** A **restriction-compatible labelling** \(L\) of \(R\) assigns to every actual occurrence \(x\) a lawful labelling \(L(x)\) of the scheme of its type, commuting with face maps: for every actual occurrence \(x\) and every face of \(x\) along which its type restricts, \(L\) of the face is the restriction of \(L(x)\). It need not be the labelling of a model, and its values need not lie below the stage. The actual labels form one such labelling; the stable labels of Layer 4 form another. The recovery statement of (R3) and (R4) is proved for every restriction-compatible labelling \(L\) whose value on the private context lies in the **prescribed bottom class**, that is, is bottom exactly at those cells below the private cap at which the private context's labels are bottom: for every realized one-point extension \(u\) on the constructed scheme, the donor face of \(L(u)\) satisfies `Correct` (3.3) against the value of \(L\) on the private context. It is then applied to the labelling that the statement evaluates. (R3) evaluates the actual labels. (R4) evaluates the stable labelling, which does not restrict to the actual private labels; only its bottom class agrees with theirs. The recovery statement for every restriction-compatible labelling uses no stable labelling and is proved with (R1)–(R3); the acquisition of the calibrated data of (R4) (3.3), its occurrence, and its evaluation come after the structural candidate of Layer 4 has been built.

The root, the private context, the donor values, and the gate equation all refer to this one occurrence \(u\): the root and the private context are its literal restrictions, the gate equation is an equation about its type, and the recovered donor values are values of its type, or of a restriction-compatible labelling, at it. Equations recorded on different occurrences, even of the same type, are not combined.

(R3) and (R4) share the constructed legal scheme, its section theorem (3.1), and the recovery statement. What differs is the relative data acquired in the model and the labelling evaluated: in (R3) the private cap and the marker are labelled top; in (R4) non-hollowness supplies an attained proper stable marker, and the data are calibrated to it. Their existence hypotheses stay separate: (R3) assumes hollowness and unbounded top-grade growth, (R4) the structural stable candidate of a model with non-hollow unbounded top-grade growth. They are not merged because their evaluation arguments coincide.

#### 3.3. `Correct`, LOW, and the recovery statements

**`Correct`.** Its data are the private cap \(C\) of grade \(N\); a marker offset \(R<N\); and three sets of donor cells: the **requested-bottom** cells \(Z\), the **exactly requested** cells \(F\), each \(f\in F\) with a reference cell \(\rho(f)\) below \(C\) and an offset \(k_f\le N\), and the **high** cells \(T\), read through the marker \(a\) below \(C\). Every donor cell outside the root lies in \(Z\cup F\cup T\); the three sets need not partition the donor's cells. For a labelling \(s\) of the cells below \(C\) (in the applications, the value of \(L\) on the private context) and a labelling \(v\) of the donor's cells (the donor face of \(L(u)\)), `Correct`\((s,v)\) holds when, everything capped at the value \(s(C)\) of the private cap:

1. **bottom:** \(\min(v(z),s(C))=\bot\) for every \(z\in Z\);
2. **reference:** \(\min(v(f),s(C))=\min(\operatorname{vr}_N(s(\rho(f)),k_f),s(C))\) for every \(f\in F\), the reference with its offset replacement, as in [Kni26, Definition 8.3.1], clause 2;
3. **marker:** \(\min(\operatorname{vr}_N(s(a),R),s(C))\le\min(v(y),s(C))\) for every \(y\in T\), a lower bound, not an equation.

`Correct` has no gate, and the cap value \(s(C)\) can be the formal top. In the hollow case (R3) the private cap and the marker are labelled top; \(Z\), \(F\), and \(T\) are the donor's bottom, proper, and top cells; the reference of a proper donor label \(\mu+i\) (\(\mu\) zero or a limit) is the reference cell of its block, labelled \(\mu+k\) with \(k<N\), with offset \(k_f=i\). Then `Correct` against the actual private labels holds exactly when \(v\) is the donor's labelling, tops included. In (R4) (the non-hollow case) the data are **calibrated** to the stable labelling of the private context, with a natural number \(\ell<N\) as the marker offset \(R\): the private cap is top in the actual labels but has a proper stable value above \(\alpha+\ell\); the reference cell of each block \(\mu\) containing a proper donor label has stable value \(\mu+o_\mu\), with an offset \(o_\mu<N\), below the stable value of the cap; the marker has stable value \(\alpha+i\) for some \(i<N\), below the stable value of the cap; and \(\ell\) is chosen so that every proper donor label is below \(\alpha+\ell\) and has finite part below \(\ell\), and so that \(\gamma<\alpha+\ell\) for the requested ordinal \(\gamma\) of (R4). `Correct` against the stable labels, capped at the stable value of the cap, then returns each bottom and each proper donor value exactly (the bottom clause and the reference clause), and the marker clause gives a stable value at least \(\alpha+\ell>\gamma\) at each donor top.

**LOW.** The setting is the residual case: a model with no rigid core and eventual top grade \(K>0\). There the acquisition supplies the private context, a donor over it that restricts literally to the requested one-point donor \(d\) (the donor of this paragraph), and the private gap below. LOW is a condition on state vectors and uses no gate, no reference cell, and no `Correct`. Its data are:

- the donor labelling \(p\), lawful, with every top cell of grade at most \(K\);
- a **private gap**: a cell \(c\) of the private scheme of grade \(K\) and a cell \(r\) below it with \(h=\operatorname{vr}_K(E_c(r),K)<E_c(c)\), such that for every root cell whose donor label is top, the entry of the row \(E_c\) at its private face exceeds \(h\): the private row's gap lies above the root tops.

A state vector consists of complete donor and private vectors \(u\) and \(v\), future fields included, and the cutoff field \(b\); there is no gate field. It is admitted at grade \(j\) when \(u\) and \(v\) are lawful on the cells of grade at most \(j\) and agree on the root, the future fields are self-visible at grade 1, \(b\) is self-visible at grade \(\min(j,K)\), and the **LOW forcing** holds: when \(j\ge K\) and \(b\) is strictly above the maximum \(M(u)\) of \(u\) over the cells whose donor label is not top, every cell \(x\) with \(p(x)=\top\) satisfies \(u(x)\ge\max(b,e(v))\), where \(e(v)\) is the value that the private gap row reads from \(v\).

**LOW recovery.** Let the admitted full-grade vector have donor part \(p\), private part the private labels with \(c\) and \(r\) labelled top, and cutoff above \(\beta_0=\operatorname{vr}_K(M(p),K)\), the grade-\(K\) rounding of the maximum of the non-top donor labels. The constructed legal LOW scheme then has a display \(w\) with donor face \(p\) and private face the private labels, and every lawful labelling \(q\) of that scheme with the literal private face and with \(\operatorname{obs}_\delta(q)=\operatorname{obs}_\delta(w)\) on every cell, for some \(\delta>\beta_0\), has donor face exactly \(p\), tops included. Restricting to \(d\) then gives exact residual receiving (R2).

**The recovery statements** have four different conclusions, with separate hypotheses:

1. **agreement below a cutoff:** in (R1), every lawful labelling of the constructed scheme with the literal private face and the bottom pattern of the whole graded index of the gate (the gate not bottom, its twins bottom) agrees with the donor below the label of the private cap, which is at least the actual cut of the private context, itself above the requested cutoff. A literal private face and a non-bottom gate do not imply this agreement from lawfulness alone when the gate has a twin: locality at the gate does not bound the gate's value from below (lowering that value to a smaller label self-visible at its grade keeps locality, by capping), and the lower bound comes only from availability against the private cap, a cell of the same grade whose scope lies in the gate's, which reaches the gate only when its twins are bottom. Gate recovery is a finite statement about rows: it uses one witness (the locality of the gate), availability against the private cap, and the orderliness of the cap, and no legality, completion, or composition of witnesses, and it recovers the donor face only (bottom and anchored proper donor labels exactly, donor tops as values at least the label of the private cap); it is still to be proved here;
2. **exact recovery of the values that are not top:** a cell whose donor value is bottom or a proper ordinal has exactly that value. In (R3) and (R4) a bottom donor value is recovered by the bottom clause of `Correct`, since the value of the private cap is not bottom, and a proper donor value by the reference clause of `Correct` below a private cap of larger value (in (R3), where the cap is top; in (R4), for stable values, by the calibration above); in (R2) both come from LOW recovery;
3. **literal-top recovery:** a cell whose donor value is the formal top has the formal top as its value, from LOW recovery (R2), or from the marker clause of `Correct` when the private cap and the marker are labelled top (in (R3), the hollow case);
4. **above-threshold inequalities:** a cell whose donor value is the formal top has a value above a threshold, possibly a proper value, from the marker clause of `Correct` (in (R4): stable value above the requested \(\gamma\)).

The third does not follow from the others. Agreement below a single permitted cutoff never alone identifies a top: at the limit stage \(\alpha\), for a permitted cutoff \(c\), the formal top and every proper label between \(c\) and \(\alpha\) have the same observation \(c\).

Each recovery lemma lists the observations it reads (the private face; the bottom pattern of the graded index of the gate; the capped readings of `Correct`; in LOW, the observations below \(\delta\)) and concludes only the donor values its first use needs. It does not require recovery of the whole type of the constructed occurrence (its auxiliary cells, private fields, or future fields), and no first use may assume such a recovery.

**The density boundary.** Three notions are kept apart, each with its name. A **cutoff observation** is the observation \(\operatorname{obs}_c\) at a permitted cutoff \(c\): receiving and the density sentence supply, for each cutoff separately, an occurrence with the donor's cutoff observation. A **stage reduction** is the reduction \(\operatorname{red}_\beta\) of a label, a type, or a realization to a lower stage \(\beta\) (`reduce`: labels at or above \(\beta\) become top), coherent under composition (`StageType.reduce_reduce`); the level observations of the full-presentation route are of this kind. **Exact extension within a specified age** is exact receiving, over every actual root, of every one-point coface in a prescribed age, as in (R2), (R3), step 6 of the top-free witnesses for top-free donors, and fullness (the section "Reduction to full presentations"). Fix a realization \(R\) at a limit stage \(\alpha\), a stage \(\beta<\alpha\) that is zero or a limit (so that \(\operatorname{red}_\beta\) is defined), and, optionally, a **specified age** \(\mathcal A\) at \(\alpha\). Its hypotheses: \(\mathcal A\) is a set of legal stage types at \(\alpha\), closed under reindexing and under restriction to closed faces, that contains the actual type of every actual occurrence of \(R\) (in the pointed case, of every actual occurrence containing the core; in the applications, an exact age: top-free, residual with top grades at most \(K\), hollow, or pointed at a named rigid core, in which case only roots containing the core are considered, and reindexing and restriction are those fixing the core). The last hypothesis is needed and is not a consequence of the others: if the actual type \(p\) of a root were outside a face-closed \(\mathcal A\), no coface of \(p\) would lie in \(\mathcal A\), and the lifting below would fail trivially. When \(\mathcal A\) is specified, the lift \(d'\) is required to lie in \(\mathcal A\), and exact extension within \(\mathcal A\) is exact receiving, over every actual root, of every one-point coface of the root's actual type that lies in \(\mathcal A\). For an actual root \(t\) of \(R\) of actual type \(p\), a **projected donor** over \(t\) (the term of (AE), the section "Reduction to full presentations") is a one-point donor type \(d\) at \(\beta\) over \(\operatorname{red}_\beta(p)\), and **exact projected receiving** of \(d\) over \(t\) is a fresh point \(y\) such that \(t^\frown y\) is an actual occurrence with \(\operatorname{red}_\beta(\operatorname{eval}(t^\frown y))=d\). The acceptance criterion of this item is: *Cutoff-by-cutoff receiving gives exact projected receiving only after proving that each projected donor lifts over the particular actual root being extended.* It restricts what may be claimed and adds no prerequisite to the main theorem: the one-block transfer of Layer 5 receives a donor already given at \(\lambda_{\eta+1}\), which is its own lift. The lifting is a named statement, to be proved, and is not derived from the coherence of stage reduction:

> **Projected-donor lifting** (a statement to be proved in each application). Data: \(R\), \(\alpha\), \(\beta\), and \(\mathcal A\) when specified, as above. *Lifting of \(d\) over \(t\)*, for one actual root \(t\) of \(R\) with actual type \(p\) and one projected donor \(d\) over \(t\): there exists a one-point coface \(d'\) of \(p\), a legal stage type at \(\alpha\), in \(\mathcal A\) when \(\mathcal A\) is specified, with \(\operatorname{red}_\beta(d')=d\). *Projected-donor lifting for \(R\) at \(\beta\)*: for every actual root \(t\), and then for every projected donor \(d\) over \(t\), lifting of \(d\) over \(t\) holds; the lift \(d'\) may depend on \(t\) (through \(p\)) and on \(d\). The single-pair form and the form for every root and every projected donor are different statements, and the acceptance criterion asks for the second wherever exact projected receiving is claimed for all roots and donors.

*Exact projected receiving from lifting* (a statement to be proved, with this argument): if \(R\) has the finite-cut receiving property, then for every actual root \(t\) and every projected donor \(d\) over \(t\) such that \(d\) lifts over \(t\), there is a fresh point \(y\) with exact projected receiving of \(d\): receive a lift \(d'\) over \(t\) at the permitted cutoff \(\beta\); the cutoff observation at \(\beta\) determines the stage reduction to \(\beta\) (a label below \(\beta\) is kept by both, and every label at or above \(\beta\), top included, is observed as \(\beta\) and reduced to top). Coherence of stage reduction is not accepted as a proof of the lifting: the identities of stage reduction relate the types of one tuple at different stages, and say nothing about which donors over the reduced type of a root lift to donors over its actual type. A specified age of legal stage types in which some projected donor over the reduced type of a root has no lift within the age is expected to exist; such an example is a separate target, not claimed here. For abstract systems of observations with coherent projections commuting with face restriction (not legal stage types), a four-point example (informal; not formalized here) has a projected donor that lifts over one actual root and has no lift over another root with the same projection: lifting depends on the root, not only on its projection, which is why the statement quantifies \(\forall\) root, \(\forall\) donor, \(\exists\) lift; it is not a counterexample involving legal stage types. The projected finite-extension rule of (AE) (the section "Reduction to full presentations") is this lifting for presentations, and the second source of (AE) there is to obtain it from its hypothesis that the two roots have equal types at \(\lambda_{\eta+1}\), one level above the comparison. Accordingly a projected structure is not called (or used as) a Fraïssé limit, or full, for an age at the lower stage unless exact extension within that age is proved for it. More generally, coherent projection of finite labels and base diagrams, receiving of every legal donor up to a permitted cutoff, and the existence of sufficiently high presentations with bounds on the levels of the presentations of one base structure are three separate assertions, none of which supplies another (`SEMANTIC_CONTRACT.md`, item 13).

**Lifting over a root of small fixing rank** (a statement still to be proved; the fixed-root case only). The **fixing rank** of a stage type at \(\lambda_\eta\) is the least \(\xi\le\eta\) such that its stage reduction to \(\lambda_\xi\) changes no label (`COMPANIONS.md`, "Fixing ranks of finite charts"); it is a property of finite labels and is not identified with a Scott rank. Let \(\alpha=\lambda_\eta\), \(\beta=\lambda_\xi\) with \(\xi\le\eta\), and let \(t\) be an actual root of \(R\) whose actual type \(p\) has fixing rank at most \(\xi\), so that its stage reduction to \(\beta\) changes no label. Then every projected donor \(d\) over \(t\) lifts over \(t\) by literal weakening: \(d\), read at the stage \(\alpha\), is a one-point coface \(d'\) of \(p\) with \(\operatorname{red}_\beta(d')=d\), and this one lift carries both the exact root (its restriction along the root is \(p\), labels included) and the exact reduction. With a specified age \(\mathcal A\), membership of \(d'\) in \(\mathcal A\) is a further hypothesis. It covers only the fixed-root case, for roots whose fixing rank is at most the lower level: it is not projected-donor lifting for every actual root, and the acceptance criterion above is unchanged for the other roots.

### The top-free witnesses: the finite age and its classical limit

Fix a countable block \(\eta\) and the stage \(\lambda=\lambda_\eta\), a nonzero countable limit. Let \(L_\lambda\) be the stage chart language at \(\lambda\) (relational, one symbol for each legal stage type at \(\lambda\)) and \(L^h_\lambda\) its definitional expansion by the hull operations (Layer 2); both have countably many symbols. The **age of top-free charts** at \(\lambda\) is the representative class (Layer 0) of the family indexed by the top-free legal stage types at \(\lambda\) of every finite arity, each read as a finite \(L^h_\lambda\)-structure (Layer 2, item 5). The top-free witness at \(\eta\) is obtained in seven steps, in this order.

1. **Finite top-free charts.** Construct the family: for each top-free legal stage type at \(\lambda\), its finite \(L^h_\lambda\)-structure, whose relations are literally its visible faces with their types. The index is countable and inhabited (the empty chart), and the members are finitely generated. Compiled in this repository (theorem named), with no hypothesis beyond the countability of the stage: `topFreeAge`, `countable_topFreeIndex`, `fg_topFreeChart`, `nonempty_topFreeAge` (`ClassicalLimit/Age`).
2. **Hereditary closure, and amalgamation and joint embedding proved directly for finite charts.** The finitely generated substructures of a member are its closed faces with their literal restrictions, which are top-free legal stage types (Layer 2, items 3–5). Amalgamation: two top-free charts with face embeddings of a common chart are amalgamated over that common face by the plain form of the coatom extension property (Layer 3, 3.1, (R6)), and the amalgam is capped at a proper cap self-visible at its arity and above every label of the two charts, which is lawful [Kni26, Lemma 2.5.8], top-free, and restricts literally to both; the square of face embeddings commutes literally, as `Amalgamation` and `isFraisse_representativeClass` require. Joint embedding is amalgamation over the empty chart. Strong amalgamation is not claimed or needed: the property does not ask the two images to be disjoint outside the common chart. These are finite theorems, proved before and without any infinite model: amalgamation is proved before any infinite model is constructed, and is not established by embedding charts into the model whose existence the Fraïssé theorem is to supply. Compiled in this repository (theorem named): hereditary closure with no hypothesis (`exists_equiv_topFreeChart`, `hereditary_topFreeAge`, `ClassicalLimit/Age`); amalgamation and joint embedding conditional on the coatom extension property `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions` at the stage (still to be proved) and on a stage that is a nonzero limit (`exists_amalgam_topFreeChart`, `exists_jointEmbedding_topFreeChart`, `amalgamation_topFreeAge`, `jointEmbedding_topFreeAge`, and, at a countable stage, `isFraisse_topFreeAge`; `ClassicalLimit/Amalgamation`). The capping step needs no hypothesis on the labels of the amalgam: it may carry top on its new cells, and the cap removes it.
3. **Classical existence.** `isFraisse_representativeClass` makes the age a Fraïssé class, and the classical existence theorem (Layer 0; available at the pin; `exists_isFraisseLimit_representativeClass`, or `exists_isFraisseLimit_of_isFraisse` once the age is a Fraïssé class) gives a countable \(L^h_\lambda\)-structure \(M\) that is a Fraïssé limit of the age. Compiled in this repository (theorem named), under the hypotheses of step 2 and the countability of the stage, from which the countability of the function symbols is derived: `exists_isFraisseLimit_topFreeAge` (`ClassicalLimit/Amalgamation`).
4. **Reconstruction of partial evaluation.** On an injective tuple \(t\) of \(M\), the evaluation is the stage type \(p\) with \(M\models P_p(t)\), and it is undefined when no chart relation holds at \(t\). The factorization of \(t\) through a representative (Layer 0) shows that the relations at \(t\) are literally those of one top-free chart at one tuple of its points, so the evaluation is well defined. Compiled in this repository (theorem named), in `ClassicalLimit/Reconstruction`, for a structure \(M\) whose age is contained in the age of top-free charts (top-free chart coverage), the limit being a hypothesis: the evaluation `reconstruct`, defined with no hypothesis on \(M\); the factorization of a tuple through one top-free chart (`exists_eq_trans_topFreeChart`), along which the evaluation is that of the face realization of the chart (`reconstruct_eval_trans_chart`, with `StageType.faceRealization`); literal recovery (`reconstruct_eval_eq_some_iff`, `relMap_rel_iff_reconstruct_eval`); uniqueness of the chart relation at a tuple (`eq_of_relMap_rel`); and the description of an evaluated tuple as the image of the points of a chart under an embedding of the chart (`reconstruct_eval_eq_some_iff_exists_embedding`).
5. **Consistency, covering, and top-freeness, each proved locally.** Each is a statement about one finite tuple and follows from its factorization through one representative: exact consistency (the evaluation of a face of an evaluated tuple is the literal restriction of its type, and undefined at an invisible face), covering (every finite tuple, the empty tuple and tuples with repeated coordinates included, lies in the points of an actual occurrence), top-freeness (every evaluated type is a member of the age), and a nonempty carrier (the one-point chart is in the age). Compiled in this repository (theorem named), in `ClassicalLimit/Reconstruction`: under top-free chart coverage, exact consistency (`isConsistent_reconstruct`), covering, for arbitrary tuples as well (`isCovering_reconstruct`, `exists_comp_eq_reconstruct_eval`), and top-freeness with every evaluated type in the age (`isTopFree_of_reconstruct_eval`, `mem_topFreeAge_of_reconstruct_eval`); a nonempty carrier when the age of top-free charts is contained in the age of \(M\) (`nonempty_of_topFreeAge_subset`); all of these, with legal types and the identification of the functions of \(M\) with the hull operations of the reconstruction, for a structure whose age is the age of top-free charts (`reconstruct_of_age_eq`), in particular for a Fraïssé limit of it (`reconstruct_of_isFraisseLimit`).
6. **Receiving, from finite pinned extension and ultrahomogeneity.** Let \(t\) be a root of type \(p\), \(d\) a one-point donor type over it, and \(\delta\) a permitted cutoff. (R5) of the table in 3.4 over the whole chart of \(p\) (the root is a whole actual occurrence, so the exact pinned extension of (R6) is the coface \(d\) itself and no coatom extension is needed), capped at a proper cap above \(\delta\) and every label of \(p\), self-visible at the arity of \(d\), gives a top-free one-point pinned extension \(Q\) of \(p\), a member of the age, that restricts to \(p\) literally along the root and agrees with \(d\) below \(\delta\) along the root followed by the new point. The chart embedding of \(p\) into \(Q\) is an embedding of finite \(L^h_\lambda\)-structures (Layer 2, items 3–5). `IsUltrahomogeneous.extend_embedding`, applied with \(S\) the finite \(L^h_\lambda\)-structure of \(p\), \(f\colon S\hookrightarrow M\) its embedding onto the points of \(t\) (the factorization of step 4: the points of an actual occurrence span a substructure isomorphic to the structure of its type), \(g\colon S\hookrightarrow Q\) the chart embedding, and `Nonempty (Q ↪[L] M)` because \(Q\) is in the age of \(M\), gives an embedding of \(Q\) into \(M\) that is literally the given one on \(t\). The image of the points of \(Q\) is one actual occurrence, and every equation of receiving concerns it: its restriction along the root is \(t\) with type \(p\), its new point is distinct from every point of the root (the embedding of \(Q\) is injective and is literally the given one on the root), and its type agrees with \(d\) below \(\delta\). For a top-free donor, a cutoff above every label of \(d\) makes agreement below the cutoff equality, and receiving is exact. For a donor containing top labels, receiving is approximation at each permitted cutoff: for every \(\delta\) there is an extension, depending on \(\delta\), serving the request at \(\delta\); there is not one extension for all cutoffs, and no top is recovered. Compiled in this repository (theorem named), in `ClassicalLimit/Receiving`, for a structure \(M\) whose age is the age of top-free charts and which is ultrahomogeneous, at a stage that is zero or a limit: `hasFiniteCutReceiving_reconstruct`, with its form for a Fraïssé limit (`hasFiniteCutReceiving_reconstruct_of_isFraisseLimit`), its form for donors on several new points (`hasFiniteExtensionReceiving_reconstruct`, through `HasFiniteCutReceiving.hasFiniteExtensionReceiving`), and exact extension of top-free donors within the age, with no stage hypothesis (`exists_reconstruct_eval_eq_of_isTopFree`). It uses the age equality (both inclusions), ultrahomogeneity, and capping, and neither the coatom extension property nor modelhood; only the existence of the limit (step 3, `exists_isFraisseLimit_topFreeAge`) needs `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions`.
7. **Modelhood, infinitude, and terminality.** The reconstructed realization has a nonempty carrier, exact consistency, covering, and the finite-cut receiving property, so it is a model in the sense of [Kni26, Definition 3.2.1] by the cap-to-model theorem (3.4). It is infinite, because a received point is fresh over a whole finite chart, not merely a new coordinate of a longer tuple. [Kni26, Proposition 4.3.23] supplies only the one-point coface: a stage type on one more point whose restriction is the given type; it says nothing about points of a realization. Freshness comes from the receiving criterion, all equations on one occurrence whose new point is distinct from every point of the root: given a finite set \(F\) of points, covering gives an actual occurrence \(t\) whose points contain \(F\); take \(t\) itself as the root and a one-point coface \(d\) of its type (Proposition 4.3.23); receiving gives one occurrence extending \(t\) literally, injective, so its new point lies outside the whole chart \(t\), hence outside \(F\). It is terminal: a model expansion to the next block changes only top labels, and there are none, whereas a model at the next block has labels in the new block \([\lambda,\lambda+\omega)\) (uniformity). Its base class lies in the loss at \(\eta\) by uniqueness of expansion and same-carrier transport, which exclude another, higher expansion of its base reduct (Layer 6). The base-reduct part is compiled in this repository (theorem named): for a structure whose age is the age of top-free charts and which is ultrahomogeneous, at a limit stage at least \(\omega\), the base-language structure of the reduction of the reconstruction to \(\omega\) satisfies the density sentence (`realize_densitySentence_reconstruct_reduce`, `ClassicalLimit/Receiving`), by legal types, exact consistency, and covering of the reduction and the descent of receiving along stage reduction (`hasFiniteCutReceiving_reconstruct_reduce`, through `HasFiniteCutReceiving.reduce`); modelhood is not used and not claimed. **Acceptance criterion of finite-age reconstruction.** An abstract Fraïssé limit is not by itself a realization. Finite-age reconstruction is complete only when its steps 4–6 prove, for the structure \(M\) of step 3:

- **literal recovery of chart relations:** \(M\models P_p(t)\) exactly when \(t\) is injective and its evaluation is \(p\);
- **uniqueness and injectivity of labelled tuples:** at most one chart relation holds at a tuple, and a tuple at which one holds is injective;

- **covering:** every finite tuple, the empty tuple and tuples with repeated coordinates included, lies in the points of an actual occurrence;
- **receiving with all equations attached to one occurrence:** the root, the fresh point, and the agreement with the donor below the cutoff all concern the one occurrence of step 6;

Local chart coverage suffices for the identification of the functions; homogeneity is needed later, for receiving, and not for the roundtrip. ### Layer 4 — Structural continuation and terminal comparisons

**Higher-stage reconstruction** (a milestone of Layers 4–5; the second of the two reconstruction problems of the section "The intended construction"). From a base realization, recover the canonical, partially defined tower of its expansions: its expansion to each stage \(\lambda_\xi\) to which it expands, on an initial segment of the stages. The milestone has five outputs, each a statement still to be proved; Layers 4 and 5 state them, and this list maps that text onto them:

1. **the structural candidate:** a realization at the next block whose labels are recovered from finite observations, the stable values of the provisional observations on actual rooted covers, built from consistency and covering, with lawfulness, exact partial evaluation, and literal reduct (the first paragraph below; `SEMANTIC_CONTRACT.md`, item 7);
2. **normalization:** every actual next-block model expansion agrees with that candidate, pointwise, undefined tuples included (the second paragraph below);
3. **a separate modelhood criterion:** non-hollow unbounded top-grade growth makes the candidate a model, by (R4) of the table of Layer 3 with its empty-root base case and the cap-to-model theorem (the second paragraph below);
4. **partial expansions:** reduction of a model expansion to a lower stage (Layer 2), uniqueness of the expansion at each stage, coherence of the expansions at different stages under reduction, and transport under isomorphism with same-carrier normalization (Layer 5, first paragraph; `SEMANTIC_CONTRACT.md`, item 9);
5. **limit existence:** for a nonzero countable limit ordinal \(\delta\), a base model with model expansions to \(\lambda_\xi\) for every \(\xi<\delta\) has a model expansion to \(\lambda_\delta\), the coherence of the lower expansions being derived from uniqueness (output 4), not assumed (Layer 5, first paragraph; condition 1 of the reduction to expansion domains).

That the candidate is uniquely determined (outputs 1 and 2) never substitutes for its existence as a model (output 3): normalization says that every expansion is the candidate, not that one exists. Finite-age reconstruction is not a case of this milestone: it constructs the top-free witnesses from a finite age, and output 4 then places their base classes in the losses (Layer 6). Coherent assignments are not models either: a family of stage types of one tuple, one at each level up to \(\alpha\) and coherent under stage reduction, is finite data at each level, and that it is the family of chart types of that tuple in model expansions of one base model is a statement about actual expansions (outputs 3–5), not a consequence of coherence. If the levels are encoded in one ambient set, the fixed-point sets of the projections are nested, and a least fixing index is spoken of only for a point that has a fixing index (the section "Reduction to full presentations", "Level observations").

   - *Five equivalent criteria* (prospective). For a base \(R\) that is a model, on a countable carrier, under the injectivity of model reduction (an explicit hypothesis until proved; above), the following are equivalent (the hypothesis is used only by 4 ⇒ 5):
     1. every supported injective tuple of \(R\) is positively nice for the family of all model presentations (hypothesis 3 of the sub-item above, with \(F=F_R\)): it has an actual positive label (a value other than `none`) in an actual model presentation at an inhabited countable threshold, and the same label in every model presentation at every higher countable index;

     3. one countable index bounds every actual finite-invariant fixing rank in every model presentation (the fixing rank of each value at each injective tuple), simultaneously over all arities and tuples;
     4. the serving indices of \(F_R\), the countable indices at which \(R\) has a model presentation, have a countable bound;
     5. \(R\) has an actual maximal presentation at a countable index.

   - *Literal uniqueness* (prospective; under the injectivity of model reduction, an explicit hypothesis until proved, used by both steps).
