# Labelled finite convex geometries and a thin uncountable infinitary class

## Objective and completion criterion

Develop a reusable library of finite closed diagrams, extension of bounded observations, countable realization, stable refinement, and infinitary comparison. Its principal application is the **specified construction**: a countable relational language \(L\) and a sentence \(\Phi\in L_{\omega_1,\omega}\) whose countable isomorphism classes number exactly \(\aleph_1\), with no perfect isomorphism antichain.

The organizing invariant is a **continuous decreasing family of expansion domains with countable successor losses and increasing logical agreement**. Do not require a total stopping rank, exhaustive rank layers, characteristic arity, a canonical terminal representative, or a Borel structure on the quotient of models.

This README is the mathematical roadmap; `IMPLEMENTATION.md` refines it into an implementation order with semantic guards and acceptance checkpoints (with a table relating its numbering to the layers below), and prevails where the two differ. `Suggested.lean` and `SuggestedInterfaces.lean` record selected Lean statements and human-owned theorem targets; proving only the statements in those files does not complete the project. `SEMANTIC_CONTRACT.md` fixes the meanings that any reformulation must preserve. `EXPOSITIONS.md` gives three informal accounts, `LITERATURE.md` the connections with the literature (with `REFERENCES.bib`), and `HULL_ALGEBRA.md` an optional development. `COMPANIONS.md` states three optional companion milestones (filtration and infinitary theory, top-free chart homogeneity, a geometric obstruction), with their Lean statements in `SuggestedCompanions.lean`; the main theorem does not depend on them.


## Reduction of the main theorem to expansion domains

Let \(Q\) be the set of countable base-model isomorphism classes and
\[
\lambda_\xi=\omega+\omega\cdot\xi,\qquad
D_\xi=\{q\in Q:q\text{ admits a model expansion to }\lambda_\xi\}.
\]
Establish, for countable indices:

1. \(D_0=Q\), the domains decrease, and \(D_\delta=\bigcap_{\xi<\delta}D_\xi\) at nonzero countable limits.
2. Each successor loss \(D_\xi\setminus D_{\xi+1}\) is countable.
3. Models in \(D_\eta\) agree on every sentence of quantifier rank at most \(\eta\).
4. Each successor loss is nonempty, by an independent top-free construction.

The first two imply countable domain complements. The third gives countable truth or falsehood sides for every sentence, and descriptive separation (invariant analytic separation and López–Escobar, in the form of the library's thinness theorem for countable sentence splits) turns these into thinness; descriptive separation is used for thinness only. For the upper bound, Scott separation (distinct classes are separated by a sentence) and the third condition make the persistent core \(\bigcap_{\eta<\omega_1}D_\eta\) a subsingleton, and its complement is covered by the \(\aleph_1\) many countable exceptions \(Q\setminus D_\eta\), so \(|Q|\le\aleph_1\). Morley's dichotomy would also give the upper bound from thinness; it is not used. The fourth gives the lower bound by disjointness of losses. The literal sentence correspondence and absence of finite models give the all-countable-carrier version. No global eventual-departure theorem occurs in this reduction.

The first three conditions do not by themselves yield the lower bound. Nonempty domains alone are not a substitute for nonempty losses. Nor does high-stage existence alone produce terminal witnesses.

### Layer 0 — General results with no construction imports

**Finite closure.** Develop finite closure operators and anti-exchange geometry, using standard notions. Prove that a finite hull operator has at most one finitary extension to all subsets, and that equality or equivariance on finite hulls extends to the global closure. The general result is reusable; the concrete global corollaries are optional for the main theorem.

**Capped extension.** For a restriction map between lawful section spaces and compatible observations, prove that lifting a prescribed face while preserving the ambient observation is equivalent to surjectivity from an observation fibre onto the corresponding face fibre. Do not assume injectivity, unrestricted caps, or that the observation itself is lawful.

**Directed finite observations.** Develop the bounded/eventually-constant versus unbounded/escaping dichotomy for monotone natural-valued functions on a directed preorder. Prove finite synchronization and appropriate cofinal-reindexing invariance. Reuse filters and \(\mathbb N_\infty\); do not introduce a new notion of infinitary convergence.

**Countable construction and comparison.** Reuse the dense-set chain theorem and the standard countable potential-isomorphism theorem. Prove only the specializations needed for finite-master conditions and rooted extendible families. Atomic compatibility of an arbitrary root is not enough: the root must belong to the selected extension family.

**Counting and observation.** Prove countability from a countable cover by subsingleton conditions; reuse the existing countable-loss theorem. Develop sentence splitting on a faithful class presentation without a measurable structure on its quotient.

**Summit 0:** these results are stated independently of the construction, and their proofs do not import it. Do not state a general result in greater generality than its two or three actual applications require.

### Layer 3 — Finite extension constructions, realization over the root, and recovery of donor labels

This layer is the specification of the finite constructions. It defines the vocabulary that the other layers, `IMPLEMENTATION.md`, and `SEMANTIC_CONTRACT.md` use for them, and it consists of four items, the checkpoints of this layer: the statements of the finite extension constructions, with the shared decoding lemma that supplies their lawful sections (3.1); one occurrence followed by labelled evaluation (3.2); `Correct`, LOW, and the recovery statements (3.3); and the table of extension statements with their first uses (3.4). `IMPLEMENTATION.md` places these items in its checkpoint order.

**Vocabulary.** Fix a limit stage \(\alpha\) and a realization \(R\) at \(\alpha\). In the applications \(R\) is a model in the sense of [Kni26, Definition 3.2.1] or, in row 4 of the table in 3.4, the structural stable candidate at \(\alpha+\omega\) (Layer 4); stage types and face maps are those of [Kni26, Definitions 3.1.1 and 3.1.5]. A label is bottom \(\bot\), an ordinal, or the formal top \(\top\). For natural numbers \(i\le N\), the visibility replacement \(\operatorname{vr}_N(x,i)\) of [Kni26, Definition 2.2.3] is \(\mu+i\) when \(x=\mu+j\) with \(\mu\) zero or a limit and \(j<N\), and \(x\) otherwise; it fixes bottom and top. A label \(x\) is **self-visible at grade \(N\)** when \(\operatorname{vr}_N(x,N)=x\), that is, when \(x\) is bottom, top, or an \(N\)-times successor [Kni26, Lemma 2.2.4]. The observation of a labelling \(q\) at a cap \(c\) is \(\operatorname{obs}_c(q)=(x\mapsto\min(q(x),c))\), on every cell \(x\).

- **Permitted cutoffs and self-visible caps** are different notions and keep different names. A **permitted cutoff** at stage \(\alpha\) is a label \(c\) with \(\bot<c<\alpha\); ordinal zero is one. Receiving and the density sentence quantify over permitted cutoffs. A **self-visible cap** at grade \(j\) is a label self-visible at \(j\); bottom and top are self-visible caps at every grade, and ordinal zero is not one at any grade \(j\ge1\). Bountifulness [Kni26, Definition 2.5.14, clause 7] and lawful capping [Kni26, Lemma 2.5.8] concern self-visible caps. A permitted cutoff need not be self-visible, and each construction passes explicitly from the cutoff to an actual cut or to a self-visible cap above it (3.4).
- A **root** is an actual occurrence of \(R\): a literal injective tuple \(t\) of some length \(n\ge0\) together with its evaluated type \(p\). The **empty root** (\(n=0\)) is the base case of every statement below; a statement that needs a root of positive length says so and treats the empty root separately (3.4).
- A **donor type** over the root is a stage type \(d\) with a face embedding \(f\) of the root's coordinates into those of \(d\) along which \(d\) restricts literally to \(p\); in the table of 3.4 it is a **one-point coface** of \(p\), a stage type on \(n+1\) points whose restriction to the first \(n\) is \(p\), unless stated otherwise. The occurrence supplying these values, an actual occurrence of type \(d\) in \(R\) or in another realization, is the **donor occurrence**.
- **Receiving** over the root at a permitted cutoff \(c\) is a fresh point \(y\) such that \(t^\frown y\) is an actual occurrence of \(R\) whose type is on the donor's scheme and has the donor's observation at the cutoff, \(\operatorname{obs}_c(\operatorname{eval}(t^\frown y))=\operatorname{obs}_c(d)\); it is **exact** receiving when \(\operatorname{eval}(t^\frown y)=d\). \(R\) has the **finite-cut receiving property** when every root, every one-point donor type over it, and every permitted cutoff admit receiving; the density sentence asserts this. Receiving at a cutoff is weaker than exact receiving, and it never yields equality at the formal top (3.3).
- The **private context** of a receiving construction is an actual occurrence of \(R\) containing the root as a literal face. It is acquired in the model by uniformity, high-arity dominance, and generalized saturation ([Kni26, Definition 3.2.1], clauses 4(b), 4(c), and 4(a)i), as in [Kni26, Lemma 8.1.1], clauses 3 and 4, with its threshold above a requested floor; the proof of that lemma cites high-arity dominance as clause 4(a) of Definition 3.2.1, where it is clause 4(c). Its type carries the cells the construction reads: the **private cap** \(C\), a cell of full scope whose grade \(N\), the **threshold**, exceeds the arity of the donor; the **marker**, a cell below \(C\); and the **reference cells**, cells below \(C\), one for each block \([\mu,\mu+\omega)\) (\(\mu\) zero or a limit) that contains a proper donor label, labelled \(\mu+k\) with \(k<N\) (in row 4 this is the stable value, and the reference of the block \(\alpha\) is the marker itself). Its **actual cut** is the largest of the ordinals \(\mu+N\) over these blocks; in row 1 the block of the requested cutoff is also requested, so that the actual cut lies above the cutoff. The gate (below) is a cell of the constructed scheme, not of the private context.
- The **master chart** of a construction is the given finite chart that it extends: the private context, or, in the countable chain constructions, the master chart of a condition. Every row, occurrence, and label of the master chart is retained literally.
- **Padding** enlarges the arity of the constructed chart by coordinates that carry prescribed labels and no request. **Freshness**: designated coordinates are new points, every cell whose support meets one of them is a new cell of the construction, and new cells are not identified with old cells merely because their roles or observations agree.
- The **bottom pattern** of a labelling records which cells are labelled bottom, as in the bottom-pattern family of [Kni26, Definition 3.2.1], clause 4(a)ii. That clause tests the pattern only on the cells of grade at most \(n\), where \(n\) is the arity of the occurrence being extended (the set \(D^{\le n}\) of the clause); a cell that a construction needs to be non-bottom on the realized occurrence (the gate of row 1) must have grade at most \(n\). A **literal-top prescription** requires given cells to carry the formal top itself, not a large proper value.
- A labelling is **orderly** when each value is self-visible at the grade of its cell [Kni26, Definition 2.3.4]. The rows \(E(\Sigma)\) of a scheme are orderly [Kni26, Definition 2.5.3], and a labelling is **lawful** when it is orderly and respects the rows [Kni26, Definition 2.5.4]: **locality**, the row \(E(\Sigma)\) transforms in the sense of [Kni26, Definition 2.3.9] to the labelling of the cells below \(\Sigma\) capped at its value at \(\Sigma\); and **availability**, clause 2 there.
- A **legal scheme** is a domain in the sense of [Kni26, Definition 2.6.1]: a scheme whose rows are consistent [Kni26, Definition 2.5.12], bountiful [Kni26, Definition 2.5.14], and complete [Kni26, Definition 2.5.15] (every graded face carries a cell; completeness is defined for bountiful rows), and coded. **Coding** is the range normalization of the rows: every row entry is bottom or an ordinal \(\omega\cdot i+j\) with \(i,j\) natural numbers, that is, an ordinal below \(\omega^2\). Legality imposes it as a condition, checked on the rows each construction builds; it is not derived from [Kni26, Lemma 2.5.13], whose offset bound is not correct as stated (Layer 1). A construction that also needs the bound \(j\le g+1\) on the entries of a row of grade \(g\) imposes that bound on its constructed rows; it is not part of legality. The recoverability that Definition 2.6.1 also asks for, that each cell determines its graded face and the plan, cells, and rows below it, holds by representation: the rows are part of the data of a scheme. In Lean legality is the predicate `Scheme.IsLegal`, whose coding clause `Scheme.IsCoded` is exactly the range normalization. A **legal stage type** is a stage type on a legal scheme; the relation symbols of Layer 2 are indexed by legal stage types.
- The **fields** of a construction are the cells of the private scheme and of the donor scheme, together with one cutoff field in the LOW construction; a **state vector** assigns a label to each field. Each construction defines when a vector is **admitted** at grade \(j\): its private and donor parts are lawful on the cells of grade at most \(j\), they agree on the root, every field of grade greater than \(j\) is self-visible at grade 1, and the construction's own condition holds (`Correct` in the growth construction of rows 3 and 4, once \(j\ge N\) and the private part lies in the prescribed bottom class of 3.2; the LOW forcing in the LOW construction, 3.3). The **catalogue** at grade \(j\) is the finite set of canonical admitted vectors at grade \(j\): normal forms, without top, whose values lie in a finite set fixed by \(j\) and the number of fields. Every admitted vector at grade \(j\) is the image of a catalogue vector under a **decoder**, a witness bounded by grade \(j\) (3.1) that sends a catalogue value to bottom only if it is bottom. The **future fields** at grade \(j\) are the fields of grade greater than \(j\); every replacement of an admitted vector at grade \(j\) retains them literally.
- **Owners**, **short** and **long-row** owners, **ownerwise decoding**, **long-row locality**, and the **section theorem** are defined in 3.1.
- A **display** is a lawful labelling of a constructed scheme, constructed as data, whose restrictions along the private and donor embeddings are literally the private labels and the donor labels. Rows 1 and 2 use one (3.2); rows 3 and 4 use none.
- The **gate** of the ordinary construction (row 1) is a cell of the constructed scheme, of grade \(N\), labelled top in the display; the **gate equation** records that it is not bottom on the realized occurrence. `Correct` and LOW have no gate.
- The **coatom extension construction** takes stage types on \(A\setminus\{a\}\) and \(A\setminus\{b\}\) that agree on \(A\setminus\{a,b\}\) (a closed face of both) and returns a stage type on \(A\) with exactly these two faces and with a cell of full scope and full grade whose label is the maximum of its labels, the apex form (3.1, row 6) of [Kni26, Corollary 4.3.22]. The bountifulness of its rows is proved for the construction chosen in 3.1 (row 6), not taken from [Kni26, Lemma 4.3.20], whose statement concerns the completion of Definition 4.3.14 and is unproved (not refuted).
- The **amalgam** of two coatom types, the **coatom extension property** at a stage, its **apex form**, and a **seed** of its completion are defined in 3.1 (row 6); the coatom extension construction is a proof of the apex form.
- A **pinned extension** of a chart is an extension whose restriction to that chart is literally the chart, labels included; it is **top-free** when no label is the formal top. The **exact pinned extension** is row 6 of the table in 3.4.
- **Restriction-compatible labellings** are defined in 3.2; `Correct`, LOW, and the recovery statements in 3.3; the cap-to-model theorem and the comparisons named in the table in 3.4.

#### 3.1. Statements of the finite extension constructions, and the section theorem

For each finite extension construction, state separately:

- **input data:** the master chart and the root in it, the donor type with its face embedding, the permitted cutoff, the padding length, the designated fresh coordinates, the bottom pattern, the literal-top prescriptions, and the cells of the private context that the construction reads (private cap, marker, reference cells);
- **compatibility conditions:** the donor type restricts literally to the root's type; the private context contains the root as a literal face, and the two restrictions to the root agree cell by cell; the threshold exceeds the donor's arity and every requested offset; the bottom pattern and the literal-top prescriptions are consistent with the donor type;
- **constructed finite object:** a legal scheme (plan, cells, grades, rows) with embeddings of the master chart and of the donor scheme and, where the row uses one, a display; all of these are data, not propositions asserting existence;
- **literal equations:** the private type and the donor type are the literal ordered face-map restrictions of the constructed scheme: its restriction along the private embedding is the master chart's scheme and its restriction along the donor embedding is the donor's scheme, with the same cells in the same order and the same rows, not merely the same lawful labellings; the root is their literal common face; the display restricts literally to the private labels and to the donor labels, and has the prescribed bottom pattern and literal tops; the designated fresh coordinates are new.

Each statement is proved:

1. for every root size, including the empty root, and every padding length, including zero; where a construction needs a root of positive length, its row in 3.4 gives the base case of the empty root;
2. with designated freshness and literal retention of the root and of every row, occurrence, and label of the master chart;
3. with bottom patterns and literal-top prescriptions given separately: neither is derived from the other, and realizing a bottom pattern does not realize literal tops;
4. with legality of the constructed scheme, its bountifulness proved at every self-visible cap of the target grade, each lift preserving the observation at its cap on every cell of the target, including the auxiliary cells the construction introduces and the future fields, even where the recovery statement that uses the construction ignores them; and with the future fields retained literally by every replacement of an admitted vector.

**Per-cap lifting, not simultaneous preservation.** Per-cap lifting is bountifulness [Kni26, Definition 2.5.14]: for graded faces \(X\preceq Y\) of the scheme, with \(j\) the grade of \(Y\), every cap \(c\) self-visible at \(j\) (clause 7: \(c=\operatorname{vr}_j(c,j)\)), and every lawful labelling \(q\) of the cells below \(Y\), every lawful labelling \(p\) of the cells below \(X\) with \(\operatorname{obs}_c(p)=\operatorname{obs}_c(q|_X)\) extends to a lawful labelling \(q'\) below \(Y\) with \(q'|_X=p\) literally and \(\operatorname{obs}_c(q')=\operatorname{obs}_c(q)\). At a cap that is not self-visible at \(j\) the lift is not available in general: [Kni26, §2.5] notes that \(q\) may then provide extra information about \(p\). Simultaneous preservation, one extension of \(p\) with \(\operatorname{obs}_c(q')=\operatorname{obs}_c(q)\) at every compatible self-visible cap \(c\), is not proved and is not needed. It does not follow from per-cap lifting through a largest compatible cap. When \(p\) and \(q|_X\) differ, let \(m\) be the least value of \(\min(p(x),q(x))\) over the cells \(x\) where they differ; the compatible caps are the caps self-visible at \(j\) and at most \(m\), and they need not have a maximum. At target grade 3 with \(m=\omega\cdot2+1\) they are bottom and the ordinals \(i\) and \(\omega+i\) with \(3\le i<\omega\); with \(p(x)=\omega+1\) and \(q(x)=\omega+2\) they are bottom and the natural numbers \(i\ge3\). When the compatible caps do have a maximum, a lift at it preserves every compatible cap, because caps nest under \(\min\); when \(p=q|_X\), \(q\) itself preserves every cap. The only known route to simultaneous preservation in general is through injectivity of restriction (uniqueness of extension), which is not available (Layer 0). The main theorem needs only per-cutoff extension: density is \(\forall\) root, \(\forall\) donor, \(\forall\) cutoff, \(\exists\) extension, and different cutoffs may use different extensions.

**The section theorem and the shared decoding lemma.** Ownerwise decoding is an explicit target, proved once. It belongs to the constructions: it supplies lawful sections of the constructed schemes in the bountifulness proofs of this item, and it is not a recovery statement.

A **witness** in the sense of [Kni26, Definition 2.3.9] is a pair \(\langle g,\sigma\rangle\) of a **suppressor** \(g\) (a non-increasing function from grades to labels, each value self-visible at its grade) and a **shifter** \(\sigma\) (a map on labels) satisfying clauses 3–5 there. Let \(g_K\) be the suppressor that is top at the grades at most \(K\) and bottom above. A value map \(\nu\) on labels is a **witness bounded by grade \(K\)** when \(\langle g_K,\nu\rangle\) is a witness. Spelled out: \(\nu(\bot)=\bot\); \(\nu\) is monotone; \(\nu(\operatorname{vr}_k(x,i))=\operatorname{vr}_k(\nu(x),i)\) for every label \(x\) and all \(i\le k\le K\); and, for every \(k>K\), if \(\nu(x)=\bot\) then \(\nu(\operatorname{vr}_k(x,i))=\bot\) for all \(i\le k\). The last condition is clause 5 of the definition at the grades \(k>K\), where it applies whenever \(\nu(x)\le g_K(k)=\bot\), and it does not follow from the others: for \(K=1\), the map that sends \(0\) and \(1\) to bottom and fixes every other label satisfies the first three, but \(\nu(\operatorname{vr}_2(0,2))=\nu(2)=2\neq\bot\). A label is **short** at grade \(g\) when it is bottom, top, or an ordinal whose finite part is at most \(g\). An **owner** is a cell together with its row; it is a **short owner** when every entry of its row is short at its grade, and a **long-row owner** otherwise.

**Statement.** Let \(r\) be a lawful labelling (the input) of the cells of a scheme below a graded face whose cells all have grade at most \(K\), and let \(\nu\) be a witness bounded by grade \(K\), applied cellwise. Suppose that every owner \(c\) is short or satisfies the **mapped locality** \(E(c)\Rightarrow\bigl(d\mapsto\min(\nu(r(d)),\nu(r(c)))\bigr)\) on the cells \(d\) below \(c\). Then \(\nu\circ r\) is lawful. Orderliness and availability are transported through \(\nu\) once, for all cells. Locality is proved owner by owner: at a short owner by composing the input's locality with \(\nu\), which is possible because short entries are unchanged by truncation at the owner's grade; at any other owner it is the hypothesis. The lemma assumes neither lawfulness of the decoded section, nor bottom reflection of \(\nu\), nor shortness of the inherited rows. Shortness is not a consequence of legality: coding bounds the entries only below \(\omega^2\), and even the bound \(j\le g+1\), where a construction imposes it, allows an entry whose finite part exceeds the grade by one. The new rows of a construction are short by their support, not by their coding.

**The section theorem.** In a construction with fields and a catalogue, let \(S\) be admitted at grade \(j\), and let \(a\) be a catalogue vector and \(\nu\) a decoder with \(\nu\circ a=S\). The construction defines from \(a\) a lawful **source section** \(r_a\) of the constructed scheme below grade \(j\). Its owners are of two kinds. The new rows, of full scope and grade at least 2, are short, by their support. The inherited cells carry the rows of the original schemes literally, and \(r_a\) is determined on them cell by cell by the values of \(a\) (the **exact base table**); their rows may be long. For an inherited owner, **long-row locality** is its mapped locality, obtained by transporting the lawfulness of \(\nu\circ a=S\) on the original rows along the exact base table, using the bottom reflection of the decoder. The lemma then makes \(\nu\circ r_a\) a lawful section of the constructed scheme whose values on the original cells are those of \(S\). This is the **section theorem** of the construction, the step of the bountifulness proof that realizes an admitted vector by a section; **ownerwise decoding** is its owner-by-owner passage from the lawfulness of \(r_a\) to that of \(\nu\circ r_a\).

Both the growth construction (the legal scheme shared by rows 3 and 4 of 3.4) and the LOW construction (row 2) are required applications: each is shown to satisfy the hypothesis owner by owner, its new owners by shortness and each inherited long row by long-row locality. The lemma shares the owner-by-owner argument; it does not identify the rows of the two constructions.

**A transformation lemma still to be proved in this layer.** The band rule of Layer 1 (a shifter normalized at \(K\), followed by the band map that sends the formal top into the band \([\alpha,\alpha+K]\)) covers only the top-witness row of the proof of [Kni26, Lemma 5.3.5]: the row \(E(\Theta)\) of a full-scope cell \(\Theta\) of grade \(K\) labelled top, the **top-witness cell**, whose witness that proof composes with the band map. The row \(E(\Sigma)\) of any other top-labelled cell \(\Sigma\), of grade \(J\le K\) and below \(\Theta\), needs a **splice of two witnesses** with the same source \(E(\Sigma)\): a witness \(E(\Sigma)\Rightarrow q\), where \(q\) is the labelling of the cells below \(\Sigma\) (the locality of the labels at \(\Sigma\); its suppressor is top at the grades up to \(J\), and the values of \(q\) are below \(\alpha\) or top), and a witness \(E(\Sigma)\Rightarrow r\), where \(r(d)=\min(E(\Theta)(d),E(\Theta)(\Sigma))\) for the cells \(d\) below \(\Sigma\), the row of \(\Theta\) on those cells capped at its entry at \(\Sigma\) (a witness given by the consistency of the rows). The spliced target is \(q\) at the cells where \(q\) is below \(\alpha\) and, at the cells where \(q\) is top, the band map of \(r\) with a base \(\lambda\) (zero or a limit) that \(r\) does not fall below on those cells, and the splice asserts \(E(\Sigma)\Rightarrow\) spliced target, on cells of grade at most \(J\). It is not a general composite: each of the two regions carries its own hypotheses. The printed proof of Lemma 5.3.5 obtains these rows through the transitivity of Lemma 2.3.14, which is not correct as stated (Layer 1); the splice replaces that step. It is a statement still to be proved here; the lawfulness of the stable candidate in Layer 4 uses it.

##### Row 6: the coatom extension property and the completion of the amalgam

**What is established.** Let \(A\) be a set of \(m+2\) points with distinct points \(a,b\), and let \(t_a\) and \(t_b\) be legal stage types on the coatoms \(A\setminus\{a\}\) and \(A\setminus\{b\}\) that agree literally on their common face \(A\setminus\{a,b\}\) (a closed face of both). Their **amalgam** [Kni26, Definition 4.3.1] is constructed as data: its cells and rows are those of the two schemes, the cells of the common face counted once, and its labelling is the union of the two labellings. Its rows are consistent and bountiful [Kni26, Lemma 4.3.2], and its restrictions to the two coatoms are literally \(t_a\) and \(t_b\), with the same cells in the same order, rows and labels included. It is not complete: every graded face whose scope is not \(A\) carries a cell, but no cell has scope \(A\), so the amalgam is not a legal scheme. Its cells, with their rows and labels, are the **boundary** of its completion. The exact pinned extension reduces to one statement about stage types:

- The **coatom extension property** at a stage \(\alpha\): any two legal stage types at \(\alpha\) on the two coatoms of a set of \(m+2\) points that agree literally on their common face (a closed face of both) are the literal faces, labels included, of one legal stage type on the \(m+2\) points. Its **apex form** asks in addition for a cell of full scope and full grade \(m+2\) that carries the maximum of the labels, as in [Kni26, Corollary 4.3.22]; the apex is what high-arity dominance uses [Kni26, Lemma 4.4.3]. The plain form follows from the apex form by forgetting the apex. In Lean the two forms are the hypotheses `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions` and `StageType.HasApexCoatomExtensions`.

From the plain form follow the exact pinned extension (row 6: one coatom extension for each point of the chart outside the face, each point chosen so that the face enlarged by it is closed), the one-point extension of every legal stage type [Kni26, Proposition 4.3.23], and the amalgamation of two legal stage types over a common face. [Kni26, Proposition 4.3.24], for the domains that are legal schemes, needs no hypothesis: the bottom labelling of a legal scheme is a legal stage type at every stage. The exact pinned extension uses nothing about the stage; the stage enters only in the proof of the coatom extension property. What remains for row 6 is that proof, in the apex form: the completion of the amalgam by cells of full scope.

**The completion: what remains, and its checkpoints.** The completion is proved in seven checkpoints, 2.1–2.7, numbered within checkpoint 2 of `IMPLEMENTATION.md`. They are checkpoints of the mathematics, and the larger ones split into several steps.

- **2.1. The seed.** Define the finite input of the completion from the amalgam: the two coatom types, their common face, and the amalgamated rows. A **seed** is that input together with the conditions it must satisfy, each stated explicitly, among them the legality of the two coatom types, the lifting properties of the amalgamated rows, and the literal restrictions to the two coatoms; the recursion of 2.6 starts from a seed. That the amalgam of two legal coatom types is a seed is proved here, from the consistency and bountifulness of its rows and its literal restrictions to the two coatoms (the library's `Coatom.isConsistent_amalgamType`, `Coatom.isBountiful_amalgamType`, `Coatom.restrictFace_left_amalgamType`, and `Coatom.restrictFace_right_amalgamType`). The **apex theorem** is stated with the conclusion of the recursion of 2.6 as a hypothesis and proved from it: the truncation to the stage (see the simplifications below), the apex, and the literal restrictions to both coatoms.
- **2.2. Coding.** The construction works under the coding of legality (row entries below \(\omega^2\), as in the definition of a legal scheme above), not under the offset bound of [Kni26, Lemma 2.5.13], the clause beginning "Indeed" (at a cell of grade \(k\), entries \(\omega\cdot i+j\) with \(j\le k+1\)), which is not correct as stated (Layer 1). Acceptance includes an input whose rows violate that bound, universe polymorphism, and the orderliness of the constructed rows derived from their consistency rather than assumed. No later checkpoint of the completion (2.3–2.7) begins until this one is settled. If the construction cannot be carried through under the weaker coding, the first indispensable use of the offset bound is identified; the legality predicate is not strengthened.
- **2.3. Transformation algebra.** Lawful transformations, normal forms, and coded encoders (defined with 2.3) and the decoders of the vocabulary above. The numerical decoding identities are stated and proved separately from lawfulness and from cap preservation.
- **2.4. Lifting and alignment.** Carrying lawful sections across grade cuts (the restrictions to the cells of grade at most a given grade) and source prefixes (defined with 2.4), the alignment of owners, and the restoration of lower prescriptions (the prescribed labels at the grades below the one being built). Each lift preserves its cap on every coordinate, and the locality of inherited long rows is explicit (3.1, the section theorem).
- **2.5. The two small arities.** The cases \(m=0\) and \(m=1\) are two separately stated constructions on their actual rows, each proved for arbitrary lawful prescriptions, with literal top, with the boundary retained literally, and with the cap preserved on every auxiliary cell.
- **2.6. Recursion on the grade.** First one grade step, from the predecessor grade already established; then the general step. Lawfulness, consistency, the prefix equations, and unrestricted lifting are kept as distinct statements; the last two are defined with 2.6.
- **2.7. The theorem.** At a stage that is zero or a limit, the apex form of the coatom extension property holds; hence the plain form and row 6. The improvement from limit stages to stages that are zero or limits is a separate lemma, with the zero stage treated explicitly. The truncation to the stage fails at successor stages (stage reduction of a lawful section to a successor stage need not be lawful), but that failure is not a proof that the coatom extension property fails there; a refutation would need its own counterexample. Whether the property holds at successor stages is open, and it is not needed.

The completion stays within the construction of lawful finite extensions. It imports only Layers 0–1, the stage types, the amalgam, and the section theorem of 3.1 (with the shared decoding lemma); it does not depend on realizations, on receiving (rows 1–4), or on any later step toward the main theorem.

**Simplifications.** At a stage that is zero or a limit, the construction completes a lawful labelling whose values need not lie below the stage and truncates it to the stage once, at the end, as in the proof of [Kni26, Corollary 4.3.22], rather than truncating every intermediate decoded value; it proves that the truncation keeps both prescribed faces literally, since their labels already lie at the stage (the library's `CellScheme.Rows.IsLawful.reduce`, stage reduction to a stage that is zero or a limit preserves lawfulness, and `Label.AtStage.reduce_eq`, reduction fixes a label at the stage). The capped-lifting calculus of Layer 1 and the section theorem of this item are reused, not re-proved. No intermediate construction that the proof does not use is carried.

**The naive completion.** The completion of [Kni26, Definition 4.3.14] has cells of full scope indexed by all lawful coded patterns (the efficient stacks of Definitions 4.3.3, 4.3.5, 4.3.6, and 4.3.7), with the rows between full-scope cells of the same grade given by the heights of their meets (clause 3 there). Its bountifulness, the statement of [Kni26, Lemma 4.3.20], is unproved, not refuted; Definition 4.3.6 needs explicit bindings before that statement is definite. Ties do propagate between full-scope cells: a tie of a lawful section at a value below the cap that is not self-visible at the grade of a full-scope cell forces equal row entries there, hence the same tie in every lawful section. Bountifulness prescribes one face, and there such a tie is already a tie of the prescription, so this mechanism separates no two cells of the face (the instance of Lemma 4.3.19 separates cells of different coatoms). No counterexample is known. The printed proof of Lemma 4.3.20 is not relied on: Claim 4.3.5 of that proof extends the prescription first, the proof then applies the joint lifting of Lemma 4.3.19 to both coatoms, and conclusions 1–3 of Lemma 4.3.19 cannot hold together under the natural readings of efficiency (Layer 1). The construction chosen instead builds the completion grade by grade over the boundary, in one fixed order of its cells; it chooses the full-scope cell that serves each lift (defined with 2.6) before extending on the other coatom, and it tops out in a single apex cell.

#### 3.2. One occurrence, then labelled evaluation

Keep three steps separate:

- **Construction:** the finite object of 3.1, as data, with its lifting properties at every self-visible cap and the conditions on every auxiliary row it introduces.
- **Extension of the realization by one occurrence** over the private context, by a model clause that depends on the row:
  - *Row 1, the ordinary construction.* The display shows that the parameter of the bottom-pattern clause 4(a)ii of [Kni26, Definition 3.2.1] is admissible. Applied over the private tuple, the clause gives an actual one-point extension \(u\) on the constructed scheme with the display's bottom pattern on the cells of grade at most the private arity. The gate has grade \(N\), which is at most that arity, so it is tested and is not bottom in \(u\): this is the gate equation. The recovery statement reads the display only through its bottom pattern, and not its values.
  - *Row 2, the LOW construction.* The occurrence comes from row 1: finite-cut receiving over the private context, in its form for finite covers, with the display on the constructed LOW scheme as donor type and a cutoff \(\delta\) above the rounded non-top donor maximum \(\beta_0\) of 3.3. Its recovery statement reads the observations of the received occurrence below \(\delta\) and compares them with those of the display.
  - *Rows 3 and 4, the growth construction.* Generalized saturation, clause 4(a)i, realizes the constructed legal scheme over the private tuple: an actual one-point extension \(u\) on exactly that scheme. No display, no bottom pattern, and no stage bound for a display is used.

  In every row, exact consistency makes the restriction of \(u\) to the private tuple the private context, labels included, and the restriction of \(u\) along the donor embedding (the root followed by the new point) an actual occurrence on the donor's scheme whose labels are those of the corresponding cells of \(u\); the new point is fresh over the whole private tuple, hence over the root.
- **Labelled evaluation.** A **restriction-compatible labelling** \(L\) of \(R\) assigns to every actual occurrence \(x\) a lawful labelling \(L(x)\) of the scheme of its type, commuting with face maps: for every actual occurrence \(x\) and every face of \(x\) along which its type restricts, \(L\) of the face is the restriction of \(L(x)\). It need not be the labelling of a model, and its values need not lie below the stage. The actual labels form one such labelling; the stable labels of Layer 4 form another. The recovery statement of rows 3 and 4 is proved for every restriction-compatible labelling \(L\) whose value on the private context lies in the **prescribed bottom class**, that is, is bottom exactly at those cells below the private cap at which the private context's labels are bottom: for every realized one-point extension \(u\) on the constructed scheme, the donor face of \(L(u)\) satisfies `Correct` (3.3) against the value of \(L\) on the private context. It is then applied to the labelling that the row evaluates. Row 3 evaluates the actual labels. Row 4 evaluates the stable labelling, which does not restrict to the actual private labels; only its bottom class agrees with theirs. The recovery statement for every restriction-compatible labelling uses no stable labelling and is proved with rows 1–3; the acquisition of row 4's calibrated data (3.3), its occurrence, and its evaluation come after the structural candidate of Layer 4 has been built.

The root, the private context, the donor values, and the gate equation all refer to this one occurrence \(u\): the root and the private context are its literal restrictions, the gate equation is an equation about its type, and the recovered donor values are values of its type, or of a restriction-compatible labelling, at it. Equations recorded on different occurrences, even of the same type, are not combined.

Rows 3 and 4 share the constructed legal scheme, its section theorem (3.1), and the recovery statement. What differs is the relative data acquired in the model and the labelling evaluated: in row 3 the private cap and the marker are labelled top; in row 4 non-hollowness supplies an attained proper stable marker, and the data are calibrated to it. Their existence hypotheses stay separate: row 3 assumes hollowness and unbounded top-grade growth, row 4 the structural stable candidate of a model with non-hollow unbounded top-grade growth. They are not merged because their evaluation arguments coincide.

#### 3.3. `Correct`, LOW, and the recovery statements

**`Correct`.** Its data are the private cap \(C\) of grade \(N\); a marker offset \(R<N\); and three sets of donor cells: the **requested-bottom** cells \(Z\), the **exactly requested** cells \(F\), each \(f\in F\) with a reference cell \(\rho(f)\) below \(C\) and an offset \(k_f\le N\), and the **high** cells \(T\), read through the marker \(a\) below \(C\). Every donor cell outside the root lies in \(Z\cup F\cup T\); the three sets need not partition the donor's cells. For a labelling \(s\) of the cells below \(C\) (in the applications, the value of \(L\) on the private context) and a labelling \(v\) of the donor's cells (the donor face of \(L(u)\)), `Correct`\((s,v)\) holds when, everything capped at the value \(s(C)\) of the private cap:

1. **bottom:** \(\min(v(z),s(C))=\bot\) for every \(z\in Z\);
2. **reference:** \(\min(v(f),s(C))=\min(\operatorname{vr}_N(s(\rho(f)),k_f),s(C))\) for every \(f\in F\), the reference with its offset replacement, as in [Kni26, Definition 8.3.1], clause 2;
3. **marker:** \(\min(\operatorname{vr}_N(s(a),R),s(C))\le\min(v(y),s(C))\) for every \(y\in T\), a lower bound, not an equation.

`Correct` has no gate, and the cap value \(s(C)\) can be the formal top. In the hollow case (row 3) the private cap and the marker are labelled top; \(Z\), \(F\), and \(T\) are the donor's bottom, proper, and top cells; the reference of a proper donor label \(\mu+i\) (\(\mu\) zero or a limit) is the reference cell of its block, labelled \(\mu+k\) with \(k<N\), with offset \(k_f=i\). Then `Correct` against the actual private labels holds exactly when \(v\) is the donor's labelling, tops included. In row 4 (the non-hollow case) the data are **calibrated** to the stable labelling of the private context, with a natural number \(\ell<N\) as the marker offset \(R\): the private cap is top in the actual labels but has a proper stable value above \(\alpha+\ell\); the reference cell of each block \(\mu\) containing a proper donor label has stable value \(\mu+o_\mu\), with an offset \(o_\mu<N\), below the stable value of the cap; the marker has stable value \(\alpha+i\) for some \(i<N\), below the stable value of the cap; and \(\ell\) is chosen so that every proper donor label is below \(\alpha+\ell\) and has finite part below \(\ell\), and so that \(\gamma<\alpha+\ell\) for the requested ordinal \(\gamma\) of row 4. `Correct` against the stable labels, capped at the stable value of the cap, then returns each bottom and each proper donor value exactly (the bottom clause and the reference clause), and the marker clause gives a stable value at least \(\alpha+\ell>\gamma\) at each donor top.

**LOW.** The setting is the residual case: a model with no rigid core and eventual top grade \(K>0\). There the acquisition supplies the private context, a donor over it that restricts literally to the requested one-point donor \(d\) (the donor of this paragraph), and the private gap below. LOW is a condition on state vectors and uses no gate, no reference cell, and no `Correct`. Its data are:

- the donor labelling \(p\), lawful, with every top cell of grade at most \(K\);
- a **private gap**: a cell \(c\) of the private scheme of grade \(K\) and a cell \(r\) below it with \(h=\operatorname{vr}_K(E_c(r),K)<E_c(c)\), such that for every root cell whose donor label is top, the entry of the row \(E_c\) at its private face exceeds \(h\): the private row's gap lies above the root tops.

A state vector consists of complete donor and private vectors \(u\) and \(v\), future fields included, and the cutoff field \(b\); there is no gate field. It is admitted at grade \(j\) when \(u\) and \(v\) are lawful on the cells of grade at most \(j\) and agree on the root, the future fields are self-visible at grade 1, \(b\) is self-visible at grade \(\min(j,K)\), and the **LOW forcing** holds: when \(j\ge K\) and \(b\) is strictly above the maximum \(M(u)\) of \(u\) over the cells whose donor label is not top, every cell \(x\) with \(p(x)=\top\) satisfies \(u(x)\ge\max(b,e(v))\), where \(e(v)\) is the value that the private gap row reads from \(v\).

**LOW recovery.** Let the admitted full-grade vector have donor part \(p\), private part the private labels with \(c\) and \(r\) labelled top, and cutoff above \(\beta_0=\operatorname{vr}_K(M(p),K)\), the grade-\(K\) rounding of the maximum of the non-top donor labels. The constructed legal LOW scheme then has a display \(w\) with donor face \(p\) and private face the private labels, and every lawful labelling \(q\) of that scheme with the literal private face and with \(\operatorname{obs}_\delta(q)=\operatorname{obs}_\delta(w)\) on every cell, for some \(\delta>\beta_0\), has donor face exactly \(p\), tops included. Restricting to \(d\) then gives exact residual receiving (row 2).

**The recovery statements** have four different conclusions, with separate hypotheses:

1. **agreement below a cutoff:** in row 1, every lawful labelling of the constructed scheme with the literal private face and a non-bottom gate agrees with the donor below the actual cut of the private context, which exceeds the requested cutoff;
2. **exact recovery of the values that are not top:** a cell whose donor value is bottom or a proper ordinal has exactly that value. In rows 3 and 4 a bottom donor value is recovered by the bottom clause of `Correct`, since the value of the private cap is not bottom, and a proper donor value by the reference clause of `Correct` below a private cap of larger value (row 3, where the cap is top; row 4, for stable values, by the calibration above); in row 2 both come from LOW recovery;
3. **literal-top recovery:** a cell whose donor value is the formal top has the formal top as its value, from LOW recovery (row 2), or from the marker clause of `Correct` when the private cap and the marker are labelled top (row 3, the hollow case);
4. **above-threshold inequalities:** a cell whose donor value is the formal top has a value above a threshold, possibly a proper value, from the marker clause of `Correct` (row 4: stable value above the requested \(\gamma\)).

The third does not follow from the others. Agreement below a single permitted cutoff never alone identifies a top: at the limit stage \(\alpha\), for a permitted cutoff \(c\), the formal top and every proper label between \(c\) and \(\alpha\) have the same observation \(c\).

Each recovery lemma lists the observations it reads (the private face; the gate; the capped readings of `Correct`; in LOW, the observations below \(\delta\)) and concludes only the donor values its first use needs. It does not require recovery of the whole type of the constructed occurrence (its auxiliary cells, private fields, or future fields), and no first use may assume such a recovery.
