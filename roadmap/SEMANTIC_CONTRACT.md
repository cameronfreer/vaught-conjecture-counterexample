# Semantic contract

The meanings that any reformulation or other formalization of the construction must preserve.
The mathematical object is fixed by the construction itself, not by the shortened prose.

1. **Logic and spectrum.** The language is relational, with one relation for each base-stage finite type. The sentence of the main theorem is in `L_{ω₁,ω}` with actual library satisfaction. Preserve the distinction between models on `ℕ` and all countable carriers; use the proved absence of finite models to identify their spectra (in the library it is derived, so far, from the cap-to-model theorem `CapToModel` or from the coatom extension property `StageType.HasCoatomExtensions` at `ω`, both of them statements still to be proved). Do not replace perfect-set thinness with a continuum inequality.

2. **Finite geometry.** Preserve the recursive visible-face plans, equivalently the proved convex geometries with exactly two extremes per nonsingleton closed set. Not every convex geometry is admitted. Two generators do not imply a two-element closed root, a bounded hull size, a linear interval representation, or convex dimension two in the literature's sense.

3. **Labels and rows.** Preserve cell scopes and grades, order/availability/locality, the visibility replacement maps, and the special bottom/top values. A cap is an observation and must satisfy the condition of its use: a permitted cutoff in receiving and density, a self-visible cap in bountifulness and lawful capping. Capping is not stage reduction. Capped vectors need not be lawful at an arbitrary cutoff; capping a lawful section at a cap self-visible at a bound on its grades is lawful [Kni26, Lemma 2.5.8], and bountifulness concerns exactly these self-visible caps, not the permitted cutoffs of receiving (Layer 3 of `README.md`). Retain the cap conditions on auxiliary rows and future fields even when the final observation ignores them. A representation of these data by variable rows (the coherent local rows of `README.md`, "Manuscript correspondence (required)", item 1) keeps the finite coded cells, the positive grades, the static semantic information (scopes, grades, and coded rows), and lawfulness, and recovers the labels literally.

4. **Actual evaluation.** A tuple's partial type is exact under face maps. Absence of a face is mathematical information. Inside a finite partial master, a supported invisible tuple and a tuple not yet supported are different states. The final closure reformulation does not erase this construction-time distinction.

5. **Model families and the sentence.** Use the original nonempty, exact consistency, covering, general-family, bottom-pattern, uniformity, and high-arity-dominance clauses; the four families remain the required extension properties. The four families demand some suitable coface; do not strengthen them to exact realization of every prescribed coface. The sentence of the main theorem is the density sentence (the structural clauses, namely injective tuples, unique labels, exact face coherence, covering, and nonemptiness, and the one-point capped-extension clause); its equivalence with the sentence expressing the four families is a required theorem, not an optional companion. Conversely, do not weaken the actual finite extension constructions to mere catalogue membership (the catalogue and legality are defined in Layer 3 of `README.md`).

6. **Receiving.** Construct actual finite rows, then an actual occurrence over the literal root, then recover the required donor labels from that occurrence (the vocabulary is that of Layer 3 of `README.md`). Keep proper-value equality, literal-top equality, and above-threshold recovery of top separate. Arbitrary lawful ambient inputs and every allowed cap are part of the finite statement.

7. **Stable candidate.** Stable values are computed on actual transported cells in directed covers. Finite stable offsets decode to `α+n`; escape decodes to formal top, not `α+ω`. Prove structural lawfulness before modelhood. Any next-block model expansion normalizes to this candidate; cap receiving proves existence separately. These are outputs 1–3 of higher-stage reconstruction (`README.md`, Layer 4); that the candidate is uniquely determined never substitutes for its existence as a model. The candidate is to be the **least** coherent (restriction-compatible) lawful next-block assignment, not the unique lift of a finite type: above a stage that is zero or a limit a type with a cell labelled the formal top has more than one lift, literal weakening being the greatest, and a pointwise minimum of lawful lifts need not be lawful (`README.md`, Layer 3, 3.1, "Least, not unique"); leastness, like uniqueness, never substitutes for modelhood.

8. **Terminal countability.** A countable cover of the terminal classes at each countable stage by subsingletons suffices. Do not require the cover to be disjoint, a canonical least core, characteristic arity, or a global rank. The grade-zero case is handled by the empty rigid core. “Rigid core” is a top-support condition, not automorphism rigidity. “Hollow” is the original no-anchor predicate, with stable-label fixedness as a theorem for models. The compiled statements of output 3 of higher-stage reconstruction, of the count of terminal classes, and of (R3) are presently formulated with cover-hollowness (`Realization.IsCoverHollow`: no anchor at the top over the compatible rooted covers), and (R4) is declared (`StableCappedReceiving`, assuming that the model is not cover-hollow) and is not proved; cover-hollowness is a distinct named predicate whose stable-label fixedness is a theorem for every realization at a block stage (`Realization.isCoverHollow_iff_forall_stableLabel_eq_top`); the equivalence of cover-hollowness with the original no-anchor predicate is still to be proved, and until it is proved those statements concern cover-hollowness, not hollowness in the sense of this item. The subsingleton step is proved one property at a time (`Expansion.subsingleton_classes_of_property`, conditional on (R1)–(R3)), and the grade-zero case goes through the empty rigid core (`Realization.nonempty_equiv_of_topGradeSup_eq_zero`).

9. **Domains.** `D_ξ` means expansion existence up to isomorphism, normalized to the same carrier when needed. Limit continuity requires the actual limit construction, with the coherence of the lower expansions derived from uniqueness (outputs 4 and 5 of higher-stage reconstruction). Successor-loss countability and nonemptiness have different proofs. A terminal witness lies in a loss only after controlling alternative expansions (in the library, by the explicit hypothesis of uniqueness of the expansions at its own block only, `nonempty_loss_of_topFreeWitness`). Uniqueness of expansions is conditional on next-block uniqueness, which is derived from (R1) and forcing donors (`Expansion.NextBlockUniqueness.of_forcingDonors`), not assumed in the main theorem. No global departure assumption is allowed in the preferred proof of the main theorem, and a statement proved under global termination on the full-presentation route is not quoted for the expansion-domain route without that hypothesis (`README.md`, "Manuscript correspondence (required)", item 5, the two counting endpoints). A sentence defining `D_ξ` relative to base models, with a bound on its quantifier rank, is a prospective companion statement (`COMPANIONS.md`, "Quantitative reconstruction" in "Further companion results"): a definition of the domain does not provide expansion existence, which the domain guard, a separate prospective theorem, is to prove.

10. **Definability.** Explicit coordinate formulas currently prove uniqueness for actual charts of a specified type with the specified extreme coordinates. New claims about generated substructures or definable closure must prove the passage to arbitrary pairs and arbitrary sets. Automorphism-fixedness alone does not supply a formula. No reverse inclusion `dcl ⊆ cl` or equality with `acl` is assumed. Preservation of the hull operations by embeddings of realizations is proved from the charts that witness them, not inferred from their definability. For finite charts both are compiled: the uniqueness of the witnessing chart (the two-charts theorem, `StageType.eq_of_restrictFace_eq_some`) and preservation by chart embeddings from the witnessing charts, default value included (`StageType.hullOp_map_of_restrictFace`). For exactly consistent covering realizations both are compiled as well: uniqueness for every pair, not only for a supplied chart (`Realization.hullOp_eq_iff`, from the two-charts theorem `Realization.eq_of_eval_eq_some`), and preservation by embeddings of realizations from the witnessing charts, default value included (`Realization.Embedding.hullOp_map`); for an embedding of stage-chart-language structures preservation is compiled only when its source has legal types (`Realization.Embedding.ofChartEmbedding`), and is still to be proved otherwise.

11. **Finite-age reconstruction from a classical limit.** An abstract Fraïssé limit of the age of top-free charts is not by itself a realization; it becomes the top-free witness only through finite-age reconstruction (its steps 3–7), whose reconstruction of partial evaluation and later steps prove all of the following for the limit structure (`README.md`, the section on the top-free witnesses). *Literal recovery of chart relations:* the chart relation \(P_p\) holds at a tuple exactly when the tuple is injective and its reconstructed evaluation is \(p\). *Uniqueness and injectivity of labelled tuples:* at most one chart relation holds at a tuple, and a tuple at which one holds is injective. *Exact partial restriction:* the evaluation of a face of an evaluated tuple is the literal restriction of its type, and an invisible face returns `none`. *Covering:* every finite tuple, the empty tuple and tuples with repeated coordinates included, lies in the points of an actual occurrence. *Receiving with all equations attached to one occurrence:* over a literal root, the restriction to the root, the freshness of the new point (distinct from every point of the root, so outside the whole chart when the root is a whole actual occurrence), and the agreement with the donor below the permitted cutoff are equations about one and the same occurrence; for a donor containing top labels this holds at each permitted cutoff separately, with an extension depending on the cutoff. *The literal roundtrip, in both directions:* reconstructing from a canonical expanded realization returns that realization, and expanding a reconstructed, locally charted structure by the definable hull operations returns its original relations **and** its original functions. Local chart coverage (every finite tuple in a finitely generated substructure isomorphic to a chart) suffices for the identification of the functions; homogeneity is needed only later, for receiving. Receiving itself uses only the literal recovery of the chart relations. The special cases are part of this item: empty carriers where the language allows them, repeated inputs, and the default values where no chart witness exists. This item is the acceptance criterion of finite-age reconstruction only; higher-stage reconstruction (`README.md`, Layer 4) has its own outputs, in items 7 and 9. Status: compiled in this repository (theorem named), in `ClassicalLimit/Reconstruction`, conditional on the limit, which is a hypothesis there: for a structure whose age is contained in the age of top-free charts, literal recovery of chart relations (`reconstruct_eval_eq_some_iff`, `relMap_rel_iff_reconstruct_eval`), uniqueness and injectivity (`eq_of_relMap_rel`, `injective_of_relMap_rel`), exact partial restriction (`isConsistent_reconstruct`), covering (`isCovering_reconstruct`), and the two-charts theorem for the reconstructed realization (`eq_of_reconstruct_eval_eq_some`); the roundtrip in direction (a) for every realization with legal types (`reconstruct_toHullStructure`) and in direction (b) for structures covered by top-free charts (`toHullStructure_reconstruct`), for the operations `Realization.hullOp`, which on an exactly consistent covering realization are those defined by the graph formula (`Realization.realize_graphFormula_iff`); the special cases in `ClassicalLimit/ReconstructionExamples`. Receiving with all equations attached to one occurrence is compiled in this repository (theorem named), in `ClassicalLimit/Receiving`, for a structure whose age is the age of top-free charts and which is ultrahomogeneous, at a stage that is zero or a limit (`hasFiniteCutReceiving_reconstruct`), with exact extension of top-free donors within that age (`exists_reconstruct_eval_eq_of_isTopFree`, no stage hypothesis); the base-language structure of the reduction of such a reconstruction to \(\omega\), at a limit stage at least \(\omega\), satisfies the density sentence (`realize_densitySentence_reconstruct_reduce`), modelhood being neither used nor claimed. Direction (b) under local chart coverage by arbitrary charts is still to be proved. *Stage reduction commutes with reconstruction* (Layer 2; compiled in this repository (theorem named), in `ClassicalLimit/Reconstruction`): for a structure \(M\) in the hull language at a stage \(\alpha\) and a stage \(\beta\) that is zero or a limit (`hβ : Order.IsSuccPrelimit β`), reconstructing at \(\beta\) the hull-language structure of the stage reduction of the reconstruction returns that stage reduction, `@reconstruct β M ((reconstruct α M).reduce hβ).toHullStructure = (reconstruct α M).reduce hβ`, on the same carrier and with the same undefined tuples (`none` faces included): the lower-stage structure of the higher reconstruction is literal. It is `reconstruct_reduce_toHullStructure_reconstruct`, a short corollary of direction (a) of the roundtrip (`reconstruct_toHullStructure`), with `hasLegalTypes_reconstruct` and `StageType.IsLegal.reduce`; it needs only `hβ`, no hypothesis on \(M\). Its general form, for a realization with legal types, is `reconstruct_reduce_toHullStructure`. Modelhood transfers afterwards (`Realization.IsModel.reduce`, which needs in addition `hα : Order.IsSuccPrelimit α` and a limit \(0<\beta\le\alpha\)), and countability enters only when the sentence at the lower stage is formed.

12. **The density boundary.** Cutoff observations (receiving and density, one permitted cutoff at a time), stage reductions (`reduce`, coherent under composition), and exact extension within a specified age (exact receiving, over every actual root, of every one-point coface in a prescribed age) are different notions. Cutoff-by-cutoff receiving gives exact projected receiving (exact receiving of a donor in the stage reduction of a realization to a lower stage) only after proving that each projected donor lifts over the particular actual root being extended: projected-donor lifting (`README.md`, Layer 3, 3.3), a statement to be proved in each application, for a realization \(R\) at a limit stage \(\alpha\), a stage \(\beta<\alpha\) that is zero or a limit, the specified age when there is one (a set of legal stage types at \(\alpha\) closed under reindexing and restriction to closed faces and containing the actual types of the actual occurrences of \(R\) (in the pointed case, of every actual occurrence containing the core), these being hypotheses; the lift is required to lie in it), every actual root \(t\), and every projected donor over the reduction of its actual type, the lift depending on both (the single-pair form, for one root and one donor, is a different statement; `README.md`, Layer 3, 3.3). Coherence of the projections is not accepted as a proof of this lifting. Its fixed-root case is a separate statement, still to be proved (`README.md`, Layer 3, 3.3, "Lifting over a root of small fixing rank"): at \(\alpha=\lambda_\eta\) and \(\beta=\lambda_\xi\), if the actual type of the root has fixing rank at most \(\xi\) (the least \(\zeta\le\eta\) at which its stage reduction to \(\lambda_\zeta\) changes no label; not a Scott rank), every projected donor over it lifts by literal weakening. It covers only roots of small fixing rank and is not general donor lifting. (R1) is receiving one permitted cutoff at a time, not exact projected receiving; its conditional form under the named hypothesis `StageType.HasCoupledGatedPinnedExtensions`, refuted at every stage above \(1\) (`CoupledGatedExtensionCounterexample.not_hasCoupledGatedPinnedExtensions`), was vacuous there and is retired. A projected structure is not called (or used as) a Fraïssé limit, or full, for an age at the lower stage unless exact extension within that age is proved for it.

13. **Three separate assertions.** (a) Coherent projection of finite labels and of the complete base diagrams: stage reduction commutes with face restriction and preserves the uniform base diagram of a stage type. (b) Realization of every legal donor up to a specified cutoff: receiving over every actual root, of every one-point donor type, at every permitted cutoff, one occurrence for each cutoff. (c) Existence of sufficiently high presentations, and bounds on the levels of all presentations of a fixed base structure (the bounded-levels criterion and eventual departure, `README.md`, "Reduction to full presentations"). None of these supplies another: a proof of one is not a proof of another, and each is proved for itself. Informal remarks, no theorem claimed: (a) does not give (b), since (a) is a property of stage reduction, true of every realization, while (b) fails for a realization without receiving; nor do (a) and (b) together give exact projected receiving at a lower stage without projected-donor lifting (item 12). (a) and (b) do not give (c): they are properties of one model and say nothing about which other presentations of its base model exist or about a bound on all of them, while (c) quantifies over all presentations of a fixed base structure (and, for the existence of high presentations, over the whole class). The remaining directions are recorded without an argument.

Completion means proving these concrete conditions and then deriving the main theorem, not constructing an abstract structure whose fields already assert them.
